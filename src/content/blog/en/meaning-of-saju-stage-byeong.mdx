Byeong (病) is the seventh stage of the Sibiunseong (Twelve Stages of Life), representing the position of being bedridden due to weakened energy. Contrary to its seemingly ominous name, the core interpretation of Byeong in Saju (Four Pillars) is the **sensitivity to understand the pain of others**.

## Meaning and Symbolism

Those who have been bedridden understand the hearts of the sick. The star of Byeong symbolizes the empathy and inner depth cultivated through such experiences. It is a stage where physical activity decreases, while the mind and imagination become more active.

## Temperament of Saju with Strong Byeong

A person with Byeong in their Day Branch (Ilji) is affectionate and finds it difficult to refuse others. They often find themselves in positions where they listen to the troubles of others, and they have deep connections to fields that deal with the human heart, such as medicine, counseling, education, welfare, and the arts.

- **Strengths**: Delicate empathy, rich imagination and expressive power, and an eye for discerning people's true intentions.
- **Points of Caution**: Excessive emotional immersion can lead to carrying the burdens of others. A tendency to worry and hesitate before starting new things is also a shadow of Byeong.

## Encountering Byeong in Daewoon or Sewoon

The Byeong cycle is interpreted as a signal to slow down. It is a suitable time for health check-ups, rest, study, and inner reflection; reckless expansion is prone to causing trouble.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Does having Byeong mean I am physically weak?**

A. The Sibiunseong stage of Byeong is not a harbinger of illness. It is merely a metaphor comparing the state of energy to being bedridden; health should be assessed based on the balance of the Five Elements (Ohaeng) in the entire Saju and one's lifestyle habits.
