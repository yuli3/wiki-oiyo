The Gye-Mi (癸未) pillar is the 20th in the sexagenary cycle. It represents the Heavenly Stem Gye (癸), a Yin Water symbolizing gentle, permeating rain, sitting upon the Earthly Branch Mi (未), a Yang Earth symbolizing devotion. It is the image of a timely rain soaking a parched summer field—a symbol of selfless dedication.

## Personality and Temperament

The Gye-Mi pillar is a devoted type with deep patience and a strong spirit of service. They shine brightest in difficult situations and serve as a reliable pillar of support for those around them.

- **Strengths**: Profound patience, sense of responsibility, and unconditional devotion.
- **Points of Caution**: Excessive self-sacrifice can lead to neglecting one's own needs. Remember to save some of your own rain for yourself.

## Structural Analysis — Myeongri Interpretation

The [Gye (癸) Day Master](/en/meaning-of-saju-stem-gye/) sits upon the [Mi (未) Branch](/en/meaning-of-saju-branch-mi/). Based on the main qi of the daily branch, the Ten God is [Seven Killings (偏官)](/en/meaning-of-saju-god-pyeongwan/), and the Twelve Stages of Life is [Grave (墓)](/en/meaning-of-saju-stage-myo/). Seven Killings is the star of challenge and responsibility—it creates a strong vessel that is tempered by enduring pressure. The position of the "Grave" indicates a structure of management, gathering, and preservation.

In terms of Nabeum (phonetic elements), it belongs to Yangryumok (Willow Wood), a classification frequently used in traditional Myeongri to group pillars with the same Nabeum.
