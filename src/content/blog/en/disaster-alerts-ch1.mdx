If your phone has ever woken you before dawn with a loud alarm in South Korea, the first thing to check is which grade of alert it was. Korea's disaster text alerts (재난문자, jaenan munja) come in three grades, and each grade differs in how loud it is and whether you can switch it off. This chapter covers those differences and the thresholds for earthquake alerts.

---

## 1. Three kinds of disaster alerts

Disaster text broadcasting is based on Article 38-2 of the Framework Act on the Management of Disasters and Safety (재난 및 안전관리 기본법), and the Ministry of the Interior and Safety's Regulation on Disaster Text Broadcasting Standards and Operation (재난문자방송 기준 및 운영규정) sets which alert is sent in which situation. The current three-grade system has been in place since 2016.

| Grade | Typical situations | Alert sound | Can you turn it off? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Emergency alert** (위급재난문자, wigeup jaenan munja) | Wartime civil air defense warnings (alert, air raid, CBR, etc.), domestic earthquakes of magnitude 6.0 or above | Loud, 60 dB or more | **No** |
| **Urgent alert** (긴급재난문자, gingeup jaenan munja) | Warnings for typhoons, heavy rain, heavy snow, storm surges and the like; terrorism; mid-sized earthquakes; tsunami warnings and advisories | 40 dB or more | Yes, in phone settings |
| **Safety guidance alert** (안전안내문자, anjeon annae munja) | Advisories other than the two above, and everyday safety guidance | Ordinary message notification (silent, vibrate or sound) | Yes |

If an emergency alert goes off, read it and act right away. That is why it cannot be switched off.

## 2. Earthquake alerts depend on magnitude

The Korea Meteorological Administration (기상청) picks the grade according to the magnitude of a domestic earthquake and whether it struck on land or at sea. The thresholds below have applied since July 2020.

| Grade | On land (inland) | At sea (offshore) |
|---|---|---|
| Emergency alert | Magnitude 6.0 or above | Magnitude 6.0 or above |
| Urgent alert | Magnitude 3.5 to below 6.0 | Magnitude 4.0 to below 6.0 (including tsunami warnings and advisories) |
| Safety guidance alert | Magnitude 3.0 to below 3.5 | Magnitude 3.5 to below 4.0 |

The area that receives the alert has also narrowed. Alerts used to go out by metropolitan city or province, so people far from the epicenter received them too; they are now sent by city, county and district (시·군·구).

## 3. Who sends them

- **Ministry of the Interior and Safety** (행정안전부): runs the whole system and sets the standards.
- **Korea Meteorological Administration**: sends earthquake alerts and some heavy-rain alerts directly.
- **Local governments**: send evacuation and action guidance for disasters in their own area.

So for a single earthquake, the "earthquake occurred" alert may come from the Korea Meteorological Administration, while guidance such as "evacuate to the school field" comes separately from the city, county or district.

## 4. Think before switching alerts off

Urgent alerts and safety guidance alerts can be turned off in your settings. But urgent alerts carry real dangers such as heavy-rain warnings and earthquakes, so if repeated notices bother you, it is better to **turn down only the safety guidance alerts**.

## Summary

- South Korea's disaster text alerts come in three grades: **emergency, urgent and safety guidance**.
- Emergency alerts sound at 60 dB or more and cannot be turned off. Civil air defense warnings and earthquakes of magnitude 6.0 or above fall into this grade.
- An earthquake of 3.5 or above on land, or 4.0 or above at sea, triggers an urgent alert; 6.0 or above triggers an emergency alert.
- The next chapter covers **civil defense warnings**, which arrive as sirens rather than text messages.
