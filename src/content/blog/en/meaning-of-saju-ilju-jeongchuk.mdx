The Jeong-Chuk (丁丑) pillar is the 14th in the sexagenary cycle. It represents the Heavenly Stem Jeong (丁), a yin fire symbolizing a candle lighting the darkness, sitting atop the Earthly Branch Chuk (丑), the ox, which represents silent endurance. It is the image of an ember that does not go out inside a brazier during a long winter night—a symbol of subtle yet persistent warmth.

## Personality and Temperament

The Jeong-Chuk pillar represents a dedicated, artisan-like personality with great manual dexterity. They silently fulfill their duties and are deeply loyal to those they care about.

- **Strengths**: Consistent talent, technical skill, and deep-seated devotion.
- **Points of Caution**: They are often poor at expressing affection through words, which can easily lead to misunderstandings.

## Structural Interpretation — Saju Analysis

The [Jeong (丁) Day Master](/en/meaning-of-saju-stem-jeong/) sits on the [Chuk (丑) Branch](/en/meaning-of-saju-branch-chuk/). Based on the main qi of the daily branch, the Ten God is [Eating God (食神)](/en/meaning-of-saju-god-siksin/), and the Twelve Stages of Life is the [Grave (墓)](/en/meaning-of-saju-stage-myo/). Eating God is the star of expression and sustenance—the blessing of making a living by utilizing one's talents is embedded in the daily branch. As it sits in the "Grave" stage, it is a structure of management, gathering, and preservation.

In terms of Nabeum (melodic elements), it belongs to Ganhasu (澗下水). This classification was frequently used in traditional Saju analysis, grouping pillars that share the same Nabeum.
