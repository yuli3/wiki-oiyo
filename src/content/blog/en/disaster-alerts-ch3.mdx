If you start looking for a shelter after the warning sounds, it is too late. The right place to go differs by type of disaster: underground is safest in an air raid, while wide open ground outdoors is safest in an earthquake. This chapter covers the types of evacuation sites in South Korea and how to find them.

---

## 1. Evacuation sites differ by disaster

| Type | What kind of place | When to go |
|---|---|---|
| **Civil defense shelter** (민방위 대피시설, 대피소) | Underground spaces such as subway stations, underground shopping arcades and building parking garages | Civil air defense warnings such as an air raid warning |
| **Outdoor earthquake evacuation site** (지진 옥외대피장소) | Wide open outdoor spaces such as sports fields and parks | After the earthquake shaking stops |
| **Temporary housing facility** (임시주거시설) | Schools, gymnasiums, community halls, etc. (including facilities also designated for earthquakes) | When your home is damaged and you need somewhere to stay |

In an air raid, **low places and underground** are safer than high places. In an earthquake it is the opposite: buildings may collapse or objects may fall, so go to **wide open outdoor space** rather than underground or indoors.

## 2. How to find them

### The Safety Stepping Stone app

You can look up shelters in the Ministry of the Interior and Safety's **Safety Stepping Stone** (안전디딤돌, anjeon didimdol) app. You can search by address or use "nearby search" to see shelters close to where you are; tapping a shelter shows its address, facility type, floor area and capacity.

### National Disaster and Safety Portal

The same information is available on the [National Disaster and Safety Portal (국민재난안전포털)](https://www.safekorea.go.kr).

- **Civil defense shelters**: choose a region under the top menu "Civil Defense → Emergency Facilities → Shelters" (민방위 → 비상시설 → 대피소).
- **Earthquake evacuation sites** and **temporary housing facilities** can also be found by region from the portal menus.

Menu names may change when the site is reorganized, so if you cannot find them, type "대피소" (shelter) into the portal's search box.

### Signs

Entrances to civil defense shelters carry shelter signs. If you notice them in advance at the subway station on your commute or at the entrance to your office building, you will not hesitate in a real emergency.

## 3. What to check in advance

- **One civil defense shelter** and **one outdoor earthquake evacuation site** near your home and your workplace (or school)
- A place for your family to meet if you are separated
- Whether disaster text alert reception is switched on in your phone (see Chapter 1)

## 4. If you are in another country

Evacuation systems and apps differ from country to country. When you are abroad, follow that country's government guidance, and also check the overseas travel safety information from Korea's Ministry of Foreign Affairs (외교부).

## Summary

- Go to an **underground shelter** in an air raid, **wide open outdoor space (an outdoor evacuation site)** in an earthquake, and a **temporary housing facility** if you lose your home.
- Find them in the Safety Stepping Stone app or on the National Disaster and Safety Portal by address or nearby search.
- Pick one evacuation site near your home and one near your workplace ahead of time.
