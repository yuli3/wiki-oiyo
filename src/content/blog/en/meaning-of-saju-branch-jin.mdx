Jin (辰) is the seat of the Dragon, the only mythical creature among the twelve Earthly Branches. It is a moist, fertile soil that holds spring rain and acts as a reservoir (水庫, water storage), embodying both grand ambitions and ever-changing versatility.

## Personality Traits

Individuals with a strong Jin-Earth (辰土) influence tend to have a grand scale and high ideals, often finding it difficult to be satisfied with the mundane. They possess an inscrutable, mysterious quality and, much like a dragon ascending to the heavens, are capable of initiating significant events even after periods of quiet. They possess a large vessel for containing water (representing talent and emotions).

## San-hap (Three Harmonies) and Chong (Clash)

In the San-hap (Three Harmonies), Shin-Ja-Jin (申子辰) forms a Water Bureau (水局). It clashes with Sul (戌), known as the Jin-Sul-Chong (辰戌沖) — a collision between the Dragon and the Dog, representing a tension between grand aspirations and the protection of reality.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. How does being born in the Year of the Dragon affect one's Saju (Four Pillars)?**

A. Having Jin in the Year Branch (年支) is considered to provide a foundational temperament oriented toward ambition and honor. However, since the Year Branch is only one of the eight characters in a Saju chart, assertions such as "being born in the Year of the Dragon makes one's fate harsh" lack a strong basis in the study of Myeongri (Four Pillars).

---

*The Earthly Branches represent the 'time and environment' in Saju. The manifestation of the same character varies depending on whether it is located in the Year, Month, Day, or Hour pillar, and the specific combinations (Hap) or clashes (Chong) it forms with surrounding characters.*
