Cheongmyeong (淸明) is the 5th of the 24 solar terms. It arrives around April 4-6, when the sun reaches 15° ecliptic longitude.

## Meaning and origin

Cheongmyeong means that the sky is clear (淸) and bright (明). It refers to a spring day when the sky is considered especially clean and luminous. Because it usually falls only about one day apart from Hansik (寒食), Korea has the saying, "Whether one dies on Cheongmyeong or on Hansik," meaning there is little practical difference between two very close things.

## Customs and foods

People visited ancestral graves, paid respects, and tended the burial sites. Cheongmyeong was also regarded as one of the best times for planting trees. The seasonal force of growth was considered so strong that people said even a fire poker stuck in the ground on Cheongmyeong would sprout.

## Saju and the solar terms

The calendar used in Myeongri, or Four Pillars astrology, moves by solar terms. Cheongmyeong is one of the 12 major solar-term boundaries (節), and **from this day, the month pillar (月柱) in Saju changes to [Jin (辰) month](/ko/meaning-of-saju-branch-jin/).** This is why the sexagenary sign of the birth month is determined by solar terms, not by the lunar calendar date.

## Wisdom of this season

A solar term when anything planted begins to grow - if there is a beginning you have postponed, the days around Cheongmyeong are a fitting time.
