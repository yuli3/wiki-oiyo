The Jeongmi (丁未) Ilju is the 44th pillar of the sexagenary cycle. It represents the Heavenly Stem Jeong (Yin Fire), a candle flame illuminating the darkness, sitting upon the Earthly Branch Mi (the Goat), which is a dedicated Yang Earth. It is the image of a campfire guarding a summer field—a symbol of perseverance that seems ready to flicker out but never does.

## Personality and Temperament

The Jeongmi Ilju is characterized by deep warmth and a persistent, "gentle exterior, strong interior" nature. Like a quiet flame, they silently warm and protect their people and their work.

- **Strengths**: Subtle perseverance, deep warmth, and a natural talent for craftsmanship or culinary arts.
- **Points to Note**: Once the embers of emotion are ignited, they burn for a long time. It is important to take time to cool down.

## Structural Analysis — Myeongri Interpretation

The [Jeong (丁) Day Master](/en/meaning-of-saju-stem-jeong/) sits upon the [Mi (未) Earthly Branch](/en/meaning-of-saju-branch-mi/). Based on the main Qi of the daily branch, the 10 God is [Siksin (食神)](/en/meaning-of-saju-god-siksin/), and the 12 Stages of Life is [Gwandae (冠帶)](/en/meaning-of-saju-stage-gwandae/). Siksin is the star of expression, food, and clothing—blessings of making a living through one's talents are embedded in the daily branch. The position of Gwandae represents a structure of upward mobility, stepping onto the stage with ambition.

In Nabeum Ohaeng, it belongs to Cheonhasu (天河水), a classification frequently used in traditional Myeongri alongside other pillars sharing the same Nabeum.
