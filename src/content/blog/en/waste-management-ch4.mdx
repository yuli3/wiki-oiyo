Do not pour gel ice-pack contents into a drain. Use the locally designated collection route for unwanted medicines. **Identify the contents before choosing a destination:** similar-looking packets need not belong in the same waste stream. This chapter concerns South Korea; it does not prescribe disposal rules for other countries.

## 1. Ice packs: filling first, collection route second

Superabsorbent polymer (SAP) gel is a water-holding polymer material, not simply water that can be emptied into a sink. Composition and performance vary by product, so a universal absorption ratio or a claim that every gel follows the same environmental pathway would be misleading.

<dl>
<dt><strong>Gel or SAP filling</strong></dt>
<dd>Check whether a local reuse or dedicated collection scheme accepts the pack. If none is available, follow local instructions for disposal intact in the designated volume-based general-waste bag. Geoje explicitly distinguishes locations with a dedicated collection bin.</dd>
<dt><strong>Confirmed water-only filling</strong></dt>
<dd>Read the product and local instructions before separating the filling and wrapper. The empty wrapper still needs sorting by material and contamination. An “eco-friendly” label does not make every paper or plastic wrapper recyclable.</dd>
<dt><strong>Unknown or mixed filling</strong></dt>
<dd>Do not cut it open or pour it away on a guess. Check the label or supplier guidance, then ask the municipal waste service if the route remains unclear.</dd>
</dl>

Reuse collection is intended to recover usable packs. An available bin does not mean it accepts every size, material or condition. Check acceptance rules rather than depositing leaking or dirty packs. Continuing to use an intact, clean pack is another option.

Expired or unused medicines require a separate collection route because discarded ingredients can enter the environment. This is disposal guidance, not advice to stop treatment; ask the prescriber or pharmacist about continued use.

<dl>
<dt><strong>Tablets and powders</strong></dt>
<dd>Follow the receiving scheme’s preparation instructions. Hwaseong, for example, distinguishes tablets removed from packaging from powders kept in their sachets. Do not crush medicines merely to dispose of them.</dd>
<dt><strong>Liquids, eye drops and ointments</strong></dt>
<dd>Prevent leakage and find a dedicated medicine collection point that accepts the form. Seoul and Hwaseong exclude liquids or ointments from their mailbox routes. Check instructions before combining contents from different containers.</dd>
<dt><strong>Supplements and empty packaging</strong></dt>
<dd>Do not automatically treat these as unwanted medicines. They may be excluded from the medicine scheme; check contents and packaging separately.</dd>
</dl>

## 3. A four-step decision path

1. **Identify:** read the pack filling or medicine form and container.
2. **Locate:** consult the current municipal, public-health-centre or community-centre collection list. Do not assume every pharmacy participates.
3. **Prepare:** confirm sealing, packaging removal and labelling. Use a mailbox only where the scheme and accepted form are confirmed.
4. **Check access:** verify hours, exclusions and leakage precautions before delivery.

<details>
<summary>Example: clearing out a bottle of medicine and a gel ice pack</summary>
<p>Do not combine them in one bag. The liquid medicine needs a collection point accepting liquids; the gel pack follows its local reuse, dedicated collection or general-waste route. A nearby mailbox alone does not establish that it accepts the medicine.</p>
</details>
