A frequent question among parents and international educators in South Korea is: **"Why do vacation start dates and durations vary between neighboring schools?"**

The answer lies in how national educational statutory mandates balance a rigid **minimum instructional threshold** with decentralized **school-level operational autonomy**.

---

## 1. Statutory Threshold: Article 45 and the 190-Day Standard

Under **Article 45, Paragraph 1 of the Enforcement Decree of the Elementary and Secondary Education Act**, public and private schools must fulfill a mandatory minimum number of annual class days (*Sueop Ilsu*):

> *"The number of class days shall be at least 190 days in each school year."*

Historically, prior to the adoption of the five-day school week (which eliminated mandatory Saturday morning classes in 2012), schools were required to conduct 205 to 220 class days per year. Following the shift to a Monday-through-Friday schedule, the statutory benchmark was fixed at **190 days**.

### The 365-Day Calendar Budget Equation
In a standard 365-day calendar year, a school's annual schedule is calculated mathematically:

$$\text{Annual Vacation Days} = 365 - (\text{Saturdays: 52}) - (\text{Sundays: 52}) - (\text{Public Holidays: }\sim 15) - (\text{Class Days: 190}) \approx 56\text{ days}$$

This leaves approximately 55 to 60 non-weekend vacation days, which school steering committees allocate between **Summer Vacation**, **Winter Vacation**, and **Spring/Grade-End Vacation**.

---

## 2. The Vacation Architecture Shift: Single Long Winter Break

In recent years, many South Korean school districts have abolished the traditional split winter schedule (4-week winter break in January, 1-week class return in February, followed by 2-week spring break).

Instead, schools increasingly adopt the **Integrated Winter Break (*Tonghap Banghak*)**:
- Students complete all 190 required class days by late December or early January.
- Graduation and end-of-year promotion ceremonies are held on the final day before break (December 30 or January 5).
- Schools enter a continuous **60-day unified winter break** that extends through the final day of February.

This reform eliminates the unproductive "February gap week" where grades were already submitted and textbooks completed, allowing teachers to dedicate the entirety of February to curriculum planning and facility maintenance.
