A school's teaching-day count is not its vacation length. One counts dates recognized for instruction; the other describes a calendar interval that may include weekends and public holidays. Subtracting weekends, holidays and 190 days from 365 does not give a reliable vacation total.

This chapter concerns South Korean primary and secondary education. Do not apply these rules to schools abroad, kindergartens or universities. Legislation was checked on 21 October 2026.

## 1. Read the minimum with its exception

[Article 45 of the Korean Enforcement Decree](https://law.go.kr/LSW/lumLsLinkPop.do?chrClsCd=010202&lspttninfSeq=82335) distinguishes school types. Elementary, middle, high, higher technical and special schools, excluding kindergarten sections, have a minimum of 190 days per school year. Civic and higher civic schools have a 170-day minimum.

For specified reasons, including natural disasters and qualifying curriculum arrangements, the principal may reduce the applicable minimum by up to one tenth, with a report to the supervising authority no later than 30 days before the next school year starts. Thus 190 is neither an exception-free absolute nor a suggestion a school may disregard for convenience. Check the particular school's revised calendar and legal basis.

## 2. Three numbers answering different questions

<dl>
<dt>School teaching days</dt>
<dd>Dates scheduled or recognized as teaching days. This is not a count of lesson hours or the dates one student attended.</dd>
<dt>Vacation date range</dt>
<dd>The interval between published start and end dates. Weekends and public holidays may fall inside it.</dd>
<dt>Individual attendance</dt>
<dd>A separate recognition question. Article 50 addresses completion, attendance and graduation rather than treating them as calendar subtraction.</dd>
</dl>

If a 365-day calendar has 190 confirmed teaching days, 175 dates remain. They are not all vacation: they include weekends during term, public holidays and other closures. That subtraction also cannot determine an individual student's progression or graduation.

<details>
<summary>Why not add four weekend days to the 14?</summary>

Imagine a vacation from a Monday through the following Sunday: 14 days. The four weekend dates are already inside that interval. Its calendar length is 14 days; counting weekdays alone gives 10, a different measure. Adding 14+4 as 18 distinct dates counts the overlap twice. This editorial example is not a typical Korean vacation length or a national average.

</details>

## 3. Does an event make a closure a teaching day?

[Article 47](https://law.go.kr/LSW/lumLsLinkPop.do?chrClsCd=010202&lspttninfSeq=82335) normally requires the principal to set closures before the school year with school operating committee deliberation. Rescheduling for a newly designated temporary public holiday has an exception to that deliberation requirement. Emergency closures and reporting are addressed separately.

Exceptional activities require prior consultation and committee deliberation. The provision distinguishes school events on Saturdays or ordinary public holidays from classes or events on temporary public holidays. When such a date enters the teaching-day count, a corresponding closure must be designated separately. A weekend event does not automatically add or remove one vacation day.

## 4. Read the actual calendar in this order

<ol>
<li>Identify the school type and school year, not another school's calendar or last year's version.</li>
<li>Find the teaching-day total, vacation boundaries and discretionary closures separately.</li>
<li>Check subsequent holiday or emergency revisions and count each date only once.</li>
<li>For personal absence, recognized attendance or graduation, consult school rules and responsible staff.</li>
</ol>

These provisions establish neither a nationwide January finish nor a fixed length for summer and winter vacations. Those claims require actual school-level evidence. Keep legal requirements separate from local practice.
