The Eulmi (乙未) pillar is the 32nd of the sexagenary cycle. It consists of the Heavenly Stem Eul (Yin Wood), representing a flexible, climbing vine, sitting upon the Earthly Branch Mi (Yin Earth/Sheep), representing devotion. It is often visualized as a flower blooming in a dry field in midsummer—a figure that thrives even in parched soil.

## Personality and Temperament

The Eulmi pillar represents a pragmatic individual who possesses a robust vitality hidden behind a gentle exterior. They are capable of building a life for themselves in any environment and are deeply devoted to their families.

- **Strengths**: Strong vitality and a devoted sense of responsibility.
- **Points of Caution**: The habit of suppressing emotions internally can harm one's health. Just as dry soil requires water, they must learn to express themselves.

## Structural Analysis — Saju Interpretation

With the [Eul (乙) Day Master](/ko/meaning-of-saju-stem-eul/) sitting on the [Mi (未) Earthly Branch](/en/meaning-of-saju-branch-mi/), the Ten God (based on the main qi of the branch) is [Pyeonjae (Indirect Wealth)](/en/meaning-of-saju-god-pyeonjae/), and the 12 Stages of Life is [Yang (Nourishment)](/ko/meaning-of-saju-stage-yang/). Pyeonjae is the star of fluid wealth, indicating an innate sense for managing money and opportunities. The "Nourishment" stage suggests a structure of preparation, where one waits for the right time to cultivate strength.

In terms of Nabeum, it belongs to Sajunggeum (Gold in the Sand), a classification frequently used in traditional Saju studies to group pillars sharing the same phonetic element.
