The Byeongjin (丙辰) Ilju is the 53rd pillar of the sexagenary cycle. It represents the Heavenly Stem Byeong (Yang Fire), the sun that illuminates all things, sitting upon the Earthly Branch Jin (the Dragon), which embodies change. It is the image of the morning sun shining over fertile spring soil—a symbol of optimism that nurtures all life.

## Personality and Temperament

The Byeongjin Ilju is characterized by a bright and generous nature. With the Siksin (Eating God) positioned in the Ilji (Day Branch), they possess both the fortune of abundance and the desire to share. They have a strong drive to initiate projects and take action.

- **Strengths**: Optimistic generosity, abundant fortune in food and sustenance, and energetic drive.
- **Points of Caution**: Be wary of the desire to show off when things are going well. The sun does not need to boast for everyone to see its light.

## Structural Analysis — Myeongri Interpretation

The [Byeong (丙) Day Master](/ko/meaning-of-saju-stem-byeong/) sits upon the [Jin (辰) Branch](/en/meaning-of-saju-branch-jin/). Based on the main Qi of the Ilji, the 10 Gods correspond to [Siksin (食神)](/en/meaning-of-saju-god-siksin/), and the 12 Stages of Life correspond to [Gwandae (冠帶)](/ko/meaning-of-saju-stage-gwandae/). Siksin is the star of expression and sustenance—the fortune of earning a living by sharing one's talents is embedded in the Ilji. As it sits in the position of Gwandae, it represents an upward-moving structure characterized by the ambition to step onto the stage.

In terms of Nabeum Five Elements, it belongs to Sajungto (Earth in the Sand), a classification frequently used in traditional Myeongri alongside paired pillars sharing the same Nabeum.
