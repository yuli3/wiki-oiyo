The Gyeong-Sul (庚戌) Ilju is the 47th pillar of the sexagenary cycle. It consists of the Heavenly Stem Gyeong (Yang Metal), representing hard iron or raw ore, sitting atop the Earthly Branch Sul (the Dog, Earth). It resembles an iron vein embedded in an autumn mountain—a figure that stands firm and steadfast in its position.

## Personality and Temperament

The Gyeong-Sul Ilju is synonymous with loyalty and integrity, embodying a martial, warrior-like spirit. With the energy of the Gwaegang (魁罡), they do not back down when faced with what they believe to be right.

- **Strengths**: Unwavering loyalty and a solid instinct for protection.
- **Points to Note**: A lack of flexibility is a weakness. Even iron must be heated to be forged into a tool—learning to be gentle is essential.

## Structural Analysis — Myeong-ri Interpretation

The [Gyeong (庚) Day Master](/ko/meaning-of-saju-stem-gyeong/) sits on the [Sul (戌) Branch](/en/meaning-of-saju-branch-sul/). Based on the main Qi of the Ilji (daily branch), the 10 God is [Pyeon-in (偏印)](/en/meaning-of-saju-god-pyeonin/), and the 12 Stages of Life is [Soe (衰)](/ko/meaning-of-saju-stage-soe/). Pyeon-in is the star of intuition and specialized talent—unique ideas and professional expertise grow from this daily branch. As it sits in the stage of 'Decline' (Soe), the structure favors building seasoned, internal substance over outward flashiness.

In terms of Nabeum Five Elements, it belongs to Cha-cheon-geum (釵釧金), a classification often used in traditional Myeong-ri alongside its paired pillars.
