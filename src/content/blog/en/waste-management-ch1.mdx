The foundation of South Korea's municipal solid waste management is the **Volume-Based Waste Fee (VBWF) System**, implemented nationwide in 1995. Known domestically as the *Jongnyangje* system, it mandates that households and businesses purchase standardized prepaid disposal bags, internalizing disposal costs to drive waste reduction and incentivize resource recovery.

However, international residents, travelers, and new households often misunderstand regular volume-based bags as universal containers for all non-recyclable garbage. Under the **Wastes Control Act** and municipal environmental ordinances, municipal waste streams are strictly separated based on downstream treatment technology: high-temperature incineration versus direct non-combustible landfilling.

## 1. Engineering Limitations of Municipal Incinerators

Most urban resource recovery facilities (incineration plants) operate continuous stoker grates at temperatures between 850°C and 1,050°C. This thermal oxidation process reduces municipal waste volume by over 90% while recovering steam for district heating and electrical power generation.

When non-combustible minerals or refractory items—such as broken heat-resistant glassware, ceramics, earthenware, porcelain dishes, glazed tiles, potted soil, or concrete fragments—are placed into regular combustible bags and fed into the furnace, severe operational failures occur:

1. **Clinker Agglomeration and Grate Jamming**: Incombustible silicate minerals partially melt and fuse under extreme heat, forming viscous slag boulders known as clinker. These obstruct stoker grate air nozzles, block combustion air, and damage mechanical drive linkages.
2. **Thermal Shock and Efficiency Loss**: Incombustible mass absorbs sensible furnace heat without generating combustion enthalpy, dropping the secondary chamber below the statutory 850°C threshold and increasing incomplete combustion risks.
3. **Premature Landfill Consumption**: Unburnt residues swell bottom ash volume, accelerating the exhaustion of regional sanitary landfills.

<Callout type="warning" title="Sanitation Worker Cut Injury Prevention">
Under South Korean labor safety regulations, broken glass, utility blades, mirrors, and nails present severe puncture and laceration hazards to collection crews. When disposing of small broken glass fragments in volume-based bags, wrap them securely in multiple layers of newspaper or cardboard, seal with tape, and visibly mark **"Caution: Broken Glass"** on the exterior.
</Callout>

## 2. The Three Primary Municipal Disposal Streams

Urban households in South Korea must route non-recyclable waste through three distinct authorized municipal channels:

| Category | Standard Volume-Based Bags (Combustible) | Specialized PP Sacks (Non-Combustible) | Bulky Waste Sticker / Digital Permit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Typical Items** | Soiled vinyl, unwashed food plastics, animal bones, diapers, facial tissues | Broken ceramics, earthen pots, heat-resistant glassware, tiles, small repair rubble | Furniture (sofas, desks, beds), bicycles, full-length mirrors, mattresses |
| **Material** | Low/High-Density Polyethylene film (LDPE/HDPE) | Heavy woven polypropylene (PP) tear-resistant fabric | Adhesive municipal payment sticker or QR barcode |
| **Sales Outlets** | Convenience stores, local grocery markets | Community service centers (Dong offices), designated hardware stores | Community centers or district government mobile app |
| **Final Treatment** | Waste-to-energy incineration followed by ash recovery | Direct sanitary landfilling (e.g., Sudokwon Landfill) | Municipal sorting/shredding centers for metal recovery and crushing |

### Potted Soil and Gardening Sand
Spent soil from potted plants and horticultural compost are natural earth minerals, but from a municipal waste engineering standpoint, they are strictly **non-combustible**. Dumping potting soil into regular combustible bags damages incinerator stoker systems. It must be disposed of in dedicated non-combustible polypropylene sacks.

### Mirror Glass vs Bottle Glass
Container glass (beverage bottles) is 100% recyclable via the circular deposit and cullet loop. However, window glass, picture frame panes, and silvered mirrors contain metal coatings and chemical stabilizers with differing melting points. These cannot be remelted with packaging glass and must be discarded as non-combustible waste.
