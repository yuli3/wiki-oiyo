The backbone of South Korea's household waste system is the **volume-based waste fee system** (*jongnyangje*), introduced nationwide in 1995. Residents buy standard prepaid bags in proportion to how much they throw away, which cuts waste at the source and pushes recyclables out of the bag. In daily life, though, the standard bag is often mistaken for a catch-all for everything that is not recycled.

Under the Wastes Control Act and each local government's waste ordinance, bags are split by how the waste is treated downstream. The regular volume-based bag in most municipalities is designed for **combustible (incinerated) household waste**. Things that do not burn go in a separate **non-combustible special-standard sack**, and large items need a bulky-waste sticker.

## 1. Engineering limits of incinerators and non-combustible waste

Most urban resource recovery facilities (incinerators) burn combustible waste at 850–1,050°C, cutting its volume by more than 90% and recovering the waste heat for district heating or power.

When non-combustible waste (broken heat-resistant glass, ceramics, porcelain dishes, tiles, potting soil, brick or cement fragments) goes into the regular bag and reaches the furnace, it causes three serious problems:

1. **Clinker and grate damage**: Silicate minerals that do not burn partly melt at high temperature into sticky lumps (clinker). These jam the gaps in the stoker grate, block combustion air and wear the machinery.
2. **Lower combustion efficiency**: The inert mass soaks up heat and drops the chamber temperature, raising the risk of incomplete combustion and pollutants such as dioxins.
3. **More bottom ash**: The residue left after burning grows for no reason, shortening landfill life.

<Callout type="warning" title="Preventing cuts to sanitation workers">
Broken glass, blades, mirrors and nails can cut or stab collection crews while they carry bags. If you must put a small amount of broken glass in a regular bag, wrap it in several layers of thick newspaper or bubble wrap, seal it with tape and write **"Caution: broken glass"** clearly on the bag. Whether broken glass goes in the regular bag or the non-combustible sack differs by municipality, so follow your local guide.
</Callout>

## 2. The three disposal channels compared

Household waste that is not recycled should end up in one of these three channels, depending on what it is.

| Item | Regular volume-based bag (combustible) | Non-combustible sack (special-standard bag) | Bulky-waste sticker / online report |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Typical items** | Food-soiled plastic film, sauce-stained plastics, animal bones, diapers, tissues, dust | Broken pottery, porcelain dishes, broken heat-resistant glass, mirror shards, flowerpots, tiles, small DIY debris | Furniture (sofas, beds, desks), bicycles, full-length mirrors, mattresses, strollers |
| **Material** | Thin LDPE/HDPE film | Woven polypropylene (PP) sack that resists tearing | Paid municipal sticker or barcode |
| **Where to buy** | Convenience stores, supermarkets | Community service centers, designated hardware stores, some convenience stores | Community service center or the municipality's bulky-waste website or app |
| **Final treatment** | Incineration, then the residue is landfilled | Landfilled directly as non-combustible waste (for example at the Sudokwon Landfill) | Dismantling or shredding, then recycling or landfill |

### Leftover potting soil
Soil from repotting or garden compost is a natural material, but as household waste it is **non-combustible**. Put in a regular bag, it damages the incinerator, so use your municipality's non-combustible sack.

### Mirrors and heat-resistant glass lids
Ordinary drink bottles are recycled as glass bottles, but mirrors (with a chemical backing), pot lids (tempered or heat-resistant glass) and crystal glassware have a different melting point and chemistry from food and drink bottles. In a glass recycling furnace they can spoil a whole batch, so they must go in the non-combustible sack.

## Summary and what to do

1. Put combustible waste in the regular bag, pressing it down so you can tie it at the marked line.
2. Put hard pieces that do not burn (pottery, glass, tiles) in a tear-resistant non-combustible PP sack, bought at a community service center or designated shop.
3. The next chapter looks at the science behind **separate disposal of clear PET bottles**, the key to raising plastic recycling quality.
