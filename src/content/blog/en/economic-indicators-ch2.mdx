A single figure such as "consumer prices rose 2.1% last month from the same month a year earlier" combines the prices of hundreds of items with the share of household spending each one takes. This chapter explains how Korea's consumer price index (소비자물가지수, CPI) is built and the order in which to read the published figures.

---

## 1. The basket and the weights

The consumer price index gathers **price changes in the goods and services that urban households buy for daily life** into a single number. It is compiled by **국가데이터처 (the national data and statistics agency, formerly Statistics Korea, 통계청)**, which publishes the previous month's index at the start of each month.

- **Base year**: the index currently published uses **2020 = 100**.
- **Number of items**: **458** items are surveyed.
- **Weights**: each item is weighted in parts per 1,000 according to the share of money households actually spend on it. A 10% rise in the price of rice and a 10% rise in rents (전월세, monthly and deposit-based rentals) have very different effects on the index. The larger an item's share of spending, the more it moves the index.

```
Index = Σ (price change of each item × that item's weight)
```

For example, take two items with weights of 100 and 20. If the first rises 2% and the second 10%, their combined effect is (100×2% + 20×10%) ÷ 120 ≈ **3.3%**. That is very different from the simple average (6%).

---

## 2. Several price measures for the same month

Besides the headline figure, the release includes several supplementary indices.

| Index | What it excludes or selects | Why look at it |
|---|---|---|
| Consumer price index (headline) | All 458 items | Reference for how prices feel and for the policy target (2%) |
| Index excluding agricultural products and oils | **401** items, excluding agricultural products other than grains, and petroleum products | Shows the trend without volatile items such as weather-driven food and oil prices (core inflation) |
| Index excluding food and energy | Additionally excludes fishery and livestock products and energy | **OECD-method** core inflation, used for cross-country comparison |
| Living-cost index (생활물가지수) | **144** frequently purchased items and necessities | Closer to how a shopping basket feels |

If the headline jumps while core inflation stays quiet, the cause is usually temporary, such as fuel or farm prices. If core inflation keeps rising instead, it is more likely to be **sticky inflation**, with service prices and wages rising together, and it has a larger effect on rate decisions.

---

## 3. What changes with the 2025 base-year revision

The price index rebuilds its base year and basket every five years. The provisional plan released by 국가데이터처 in July 2026 is as follows.

- **Base year**: 2020 → **2025**
- **Number of items**: 458 → **455** (10 added, 13 removed)
- **Examples added**: smartwatches, electric vehicle charging fees, software subscription fees, cloud storage fees, malatang, salads
- **Examples removed**: bracken (고사리), bellflower root (도라지), peanuts, dashcams (블랙박스), lunch boxes (도시락), kindergarten fees
- **Weights**: the share of services grows (552.4 → 578.2) and the share of goods shrinks (447.6 → 421.8).
- **Schedule**: the revision results are due on December 18, 2026, and the new-base index is to be applied from the consumer price release of December 31. Index levels and inflation rates for past periods will also be recalculated on the new base and may differ slightly.

<Callout type="important" title="When comparing figures from before and after the revision">
Right after the revision, news articles, older reports and the past inflation rates in the new statistical tables may differ slightly from one another. It is safer to compare tables that use the same base year and to note the base year of each source.
</Callout>

## 4. The order to read the release

1. **Year-on-year inflation**: the headline. Compare it with the Bank of Korea's 2% target.
2. **The two core measures**: check whether they move in the same direction as the headline.
3. **Top contributing items**: which of petroleum products, agricultural, livestock and fishery products, eating out, or rents pushed prices up.
4. **Month-on-month inflation**: whether the recent pace is speeding up or slowing down. Seasonal factors (kimchi-making season, holidays) are mixed in, so compare the same months.
5. **Inflation expectations**: if expected inflation in the Bank of Korea's Consumer Survey (소비자동향조사) rises at the same time, it is read as a sign that inflation will not come down easily.

---

## Summary

The consumer price index is a weighted average of the prices of 458 items, weighted by their share of household spending. Look at core inflation and the contributing items alongside the headline to tell a temporary rise from a trend. At the end of 2026, the 2025 base-year revision will change both the basket and past inflation rates.
