South Korea's Ministry of Environment rolled out the **separate disposal of clear PET bottles** in apartment complexes in December 2020 and in detached houses nationwide in December 2021. Before that, all plastics were collected together. Now colorless, transparent water and drink bottles must be kept apart from general plastics and put in a dedicated bin.

It looks like just one more sorting category, but it reflects how polymer chemistry and the recycling supply chain work.

---

## 1. Filament yarn versus staple fiber

Under mixed collection, clear PET bottles were collected together with colored PET (green soda bottles, brown beer bottles), multi-layer plastics (OTHER) and dirt. Even after shredding into recycled flakes, the color was uneven and the material weaker, so it was mostly **downcycled** into low-grade **staple fiber** for stuffing, twine and non-woven fabric.

Clean, colorless PET bottles can be purified into **filament yarn**, a high-value fiber for clothing, outdoor wear, bags and running-shoe uppers. In the end they can even become new bottles again, closing the **bottle-to-bottle** loop.

```
[Mixed PET collection] ───> low-purity flakes ───> non-woven fabric/egg trays/twine (staple fiber: limited reuse)
[Clear PET separated]  ───> high-purity pellets ──> clothing/shoes/new bottles (filament & bottle-to-bottle)
```

Clear PET bottles go out following the Ministry of Environment's four common steps.

1. **Empty**: Pour out all the contents.
2. **Rinse**: Lightly rinse the inside with water to wash away drink residue.
3. **Remove the label**: Peel the plastic label (mostly PP or PS) completely along the perforation and put it out with plastic film.
4. **Crush and close the cap**: Flatten the bottle, then screw the cap back on.

<Callout type="tip" title="The cap isn't PET, so why put it back on?">
Many people think caps and neck rings should be thrown away separately because they are made of polyethylene (PE) or polypropylene (PP). But **the official guidance is to close the cap**, for two reasons:
1. **Keeps the bottle flat and clean**: A closed cap keeps the bottle compressed and stops sand or dirt getting inside during collection and sorting.
2. **Float-sink separation in water**: Recycling plants shred bottles and caps together and drop them into water.
   - PET has a specific gravity of about **1.3–1.4**, heavier than water (1.0), so it **sinks**.
   - Caps (PE/PP) are about **0.90–0.95**, lighter than water, so they **float**.
   The wash tank separates most of the cap material from the bottle material mechanically.
</Callout>

## 3. What not to put in the clear PET bin

Not every clear plastic container belongs in the clear PET bin. Only **colorless bottles that held drinking water or beverages** qualify.

| Item | Material and features | Correct channel |
|---|---|---|
| **Clear water, sparkling water and drink bottles** | Colorless polyethylene terephthalate (PET) | **Dedicated clear PET bin** |
| **Disposable clear takeout cups** | PS (polystyrene) or PET of mixed grades | **General plastics** |
| **Clear fruit and salad trays** | Mostly extruded sheet (PET/OTHER) | **General plastics** |
| **Clear sauce, soy sauce and cooking-oil bottles** | Oil or concentrated sauce soaks in and can't be washed out | **General plastics (regular bag if badly soiled)** |
| **Colored PET bottles (green, brown, etc.)** | PET with added dye | **General plastics** |

## Summary

Sorted correctly, clear PET bottles can become top-grade fiber without any new fossil-based raw material.

The key steps are **removing the label** and **crushing the bottle and closing the cap**. Chapter 3 covers the line between **food waste and general waste**, the most confusing call at home, from the viewpoint of how food waste becomes animal feed.
