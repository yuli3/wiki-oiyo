Jeong-in (正印) means the "Right Seal"—it is the orthodox energy that nurtures the self. It is a representative auspicious star (Gil-sin) symbolizing a mother's love, a teacher's guidance, academic degrees, professional qualifications, and the power of receptivity.

## At a Glance

| Category | Description |
|---|---|
| Definition | The element that generates the self (生) with a different yin-yang polarity |
| Governed Areas | Academics, receptivity, motherhood, qualifications |
| Core Keywords | Learning ability, receptivity, stability, benevolence (virtue from others) |

## Personality Manifestation

Those with a prominent Jeong-in enjoy learning, possess deep understanding, and frequently receive help from elders and mentors (noble people). They are generous, conservative, and prioritize spiritual values over material ones.

## In Career and Work

It is the primary Sipsin for tracks where knowledge and qualifications form the foundation, such as academics, education, publishing, research, and the public sector.

## In Wealth

Rather than directly chasing money, it follows a structure where wealth naturally follows once one has cultivated their skills and qualifications. It has a deep connection to "document luck" (contracts, real estate, certifications).

## Excess and Deficiency

When excessive, one becomes accustomed to receiving, leading to dependency and a tendency to overthink, which suppresses action (Siksang). When deficient, one lacks the necessary rest and study time to replenish oneself.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. My Jeong-in is strong, but people call me lazy. Why is that?**

A. An excess of Jeong-in means your "charging mode" is the default state, which may appear as laziness to others. Check if your structure is blocked in terms of output (Siksang) compared to your input (study). Establishing a small output routine, such as teaching what you have learned or writing it down, will help restore balance.

*Sipsin is a language for interpreting the relationship between other characters based on the Day Master (the self). The manifestation of the same Sipsin varies depending on its position, strength, and interactions (Hap/Chung); there is no such thing as an inherently "good" or "bad" Sipsin.*
