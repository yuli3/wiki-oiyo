In modern civic life, time is governed by several overlapping calendar cycles: the **Civil Calendar Year** (January 1 to December 31), the **Fiscal Year** for state budgeting, and the **Academic Year (School Year, 학년도)** for public education.

In South Korea, while the civic calendar begins in January, every primary, middle, and high school operates on a legally defined cycle that begins on **March 1st** and concludes on the **final day of February of the following year**.

---

## 1. Statutory Foundation: Article 24 of the Education Act

The legal boundary of South Korea's academic cycle is codified in **Article 24, Paragraph 1 of the Elementary and Secondary Education Act**:

> *"The school year shall begin on March 1 and end on the last day of February of the following year."*

This statutory formulation generates a distinctive administrative reality:

1. **Two-Semester Architecture**:
   - **Semester 1**: March 1 through late August (approximately 20 to 21 instructional weeks).
   - **Semester 2**: Late August through the end of February (divided by winter break and grade-end transitional breaks).
2. **Student Grade Status**: A student remains legally enrolled in their current grade through the end of February. Graduation ceremonies and matriculation announcements conducted in early February do not officially change a student's grade level until March 1st arrives.

---

## 2. Historical Evolution: From April to September to March

South Korea's March school year is the historical product of post-liberation administrative reforms:

| Era / Year | School Year Start Date | Historical and Administrative Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Japanese Colonial Era (~1945)** | April 1st | Mirrored Imperial Japanese fiscal and educational cycles (sakura spring start) |
| **Post-Liberation & USAMGIK (1946~1949)** | **September 1st** | United States Army Military Government instituted a fall start matching the American school system |
| **First Republic Reform (1950)** | April 1st | Reverted to an early spring cycle to avoid harsh winter classroom heating deficits |
| **Third Republic Consolidation (1962~Present)** | **March 1st** | Unified by Education Law Amendment (Law No. 811) to maximize spring instructional days and align with agricultural labor patterns |

The selection of March avoided freezing January and February weather in era when rural schoolhouses lacked central heating, while granting teachers a two-week transitional window in late February to finalize grade transcripts and organize new homeroom rosters.
