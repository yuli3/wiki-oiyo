**An academic year, a semester, an accounting year and a person's graduation date answer different questions.** Matching dates do not make their legal effects identical. This chapter concerns South Korea's elementary and secondary school framework, not a universal rule for universities, kindergartens or schools abroad.

Rules checked: **2026-10-19**, against the current official legislation below. This chapter does not determine an individual student's status or provide a particular school's live calendar.

## 1. The statutory year is not the first day of attendance

[Article 24(1) of the Elementary and Secondary Education Act](https://law.go.kr/LSW/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lspttninfSeq=147073) sets the school year from **March 1 through the last day of the following February**. The current Korean article heading is “수업 등”. Do not append the semester calendar as though it were a quotation of Article 24(2).

The year's legal start does not mean pupils attend or hold an entrance ceremony that day. Check the school's actual dates for attendance, entrance ceremonies, end-of-classes events and graduation ceremonies. Holidays and local scheduling can change them; there is no nationwide guarantee that every first attendance date is March 2.

### A date-reading example

The 2026 academic year runs from March 1, 2026 through February 28, 2027. January 2027 therefore belongs to calendar year 2027 but academic year 2026 in this framework. This locates a date within a period; it does not establish whether a particular person is enrolled or has graduated. If the relevant February is in a leap year, its last day differs, so not every academic year is exactly 365 days.

## 2. Semesters require both the decree and the school's decision

[Article 44 of the Enforcement Decree](https://law.go.kr/LSW/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lspttninfSeq=82335) normally divides the academic year into two semesters.

Paragraph 2 also provides semester exceptions for specified autonomous schools under the prescribed procedure. A general rule is not an exception-free national calendar. Read the applicable rules alongside the school's published annual schedule.

A semester's calendar length is not its number of instructional days. Vacation can fall within a semester. A description such as “a twenty-week semester” does not by itself establish statutory class days; those require a separate rule and count.

## 3. School accounts share the dates but serve a different purpose

[Articles 30-2 and 30-3 of the Act](https://law.go.kr/LSW/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lspttninfSeq=147073) govern the school-account framework for national and public schools. Article 30-3(1) sets its accounting year from **March 1 through the last day of the following February**.

The academic-year period concerns education; the school-account year concerns budget revenues, expenditure and accounting. Matching periods do not determine graduation or a student's entitlement to a fare. Do not generalize this provision to every private institution or university account.

A school's own account and an education authority's [education special account](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsSideInfoP.do?docCls=jo&joBrNo=00&joNo=0038&lsiSeq=279639&urlMode=lsScJoRltInfoR) are also different categories with different governing provisions. The school-account March–February rule is not enough to assign that period to every education-related account. First identify the institution and the particular account being compared.

## 4. Year-end is not every individual's graduation date

[Article 50 of the Enforcement Decree](https://law.go.kr/LSW/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lspttninfSeq=82335) separately addresses the principal's assessment of curriculum completion and recognition of completion or graduation. The academic-year provision alone does not establish that someone graduating in January must retain the same enrolment status until the end of February.

School accident compensation, student-card validity and youth fares each require their own applicable conditions. This chapter decides neither compensation eligibility nor a particular fare entitlement. Where needed, ask the school's records office or the institution administering the scheme, providing the relevant dates and documentation.

## Summary

Identify the question first. An academic year requires the statutory period; a semester requires the decree and school calendar; an accounting year requires the particular account's rules; an individual's graduation requires the relevant decision and record. Do not use one period as a substitute for another scheme's legal conditions.
