The Byeong-O (丙午) Ilju is the 43rd pillar of the sexagenary cycle. It represents the Heavenly Stem of Byeong (Yang Fire), the sun that illuminates all things, sitting atop the Earthly Branch of O (the Horse), the symbol of passion. It is the image of the sun at high noon, receiving the full energy of the peak of the Twelve Stages of Life (십이운성).

## Personality and Temperament

The Byeong-O Ilju is a "sun-type" personality with a level of passion and presence that ranks among the highest in the sexagenary cycle. With the intense energy of the Yang-in (羊刃), they lead with fervor and take responsibility with equal intensity.

- **Strengths**: Overwhelming passion and presence, refreshing and decisive leadership.
- **Points of Caution**: If you burn everything, you will eventually burn yourself. The brighter the noon sun, the more necessary it is to create shade.

## Structural Analysis — Saju Interpretation

The [Byeong (丙) Day Master](/ko/meaning-of-saju-stem-byeong/) sits upon the [O (午) Earthly Branch](/en/meaning-of-saju-branch-o/). Based on the main qi of the Ilji, the Ten God is [Geopjae (劫財)](/en/meaning-of-saju-god-geopjae/), and the Twelve Stages of Life is [Jewang (帝旺)](/ko/meaning-of-saju-stage-jewang/). Geopjae is the star of competition and ambition—it thrives in rivalry, but also brings fluctuations in wealth. As it sits in the position of the Emperor (Jewang), it is a strong and prosperous structure where energy reaches its zenith.

In terms of Nabeum Five Elements, it belongs to Cheonhasu (天河水, Heavenly River Water), a classification often used in traditional Saju studies to group pillars sharing the same Nabeum.
