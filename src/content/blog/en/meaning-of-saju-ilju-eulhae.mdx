The Eulhae (乙亥) pillar is the 12th entry in the Sexagenary Cycle. It represents the Heavenly Stem Eul—Yin Wood, symbolizing a flexible, climbing vine—sitting upon the Earthly Branch Hae, the abundant Pig. It is the image of a lotus flower blooming on a winter sea—a symbol of compassion rooted in clear, deep water.

## Personality and Temperament

The Eulhae pillar represents a seeker-type personality with a rich imagination and a warm heart. They have a deep affinity for scholarship, religion, and the arts, and they find fulfillment in helping others.

- **Strengths**: Rich imagination, strong learning ability, a generous nature, and spiritual depth.
- **Points to Note**: They are prone to dwelling in dreams rather than reality. They require routines that keep their feet firmly on the ground.

## Structural Reading — Myeongri Interpretation

With the [Eul (乙) Day Master](/en/meaning-of-saju-stem-eul/) sitting atop the [Hae (亥) Branch](/en/meaning-of-saju-branch-hae/), the Ten God (Shipsin) based on the main qi of the daily branch is [Jeongin (正印)](/en/meaning-of-saju-god-jeongin/), and the 12 Stages of Life (Shipiunseong) is [Sa (死)](/en/meaning-of-saju-stage-sa/). The Jeongin is the star of scholarship and receptivity—the foundation of the ability to learn, absorb, and receive the benevolence of others. Because it sits in the "Sa" (Stillness) stage, it is a structure characterized by deep immersion in a single field.

In terms of Nabeum (phonetic element), it belongs to Sanduhwa (Mountain-top Fire), a classification often used in traditional Myeongri to group pillars with matching Nabeum.
