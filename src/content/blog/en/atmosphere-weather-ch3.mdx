**An apparent-temperature index is not a thermometer corrected to reveal the body's true temperature.** Wind chill, heat index and WBGT summarize different conditions. Shared temperature units do not make them interchangeable or turn them into skin or core-temperature measurements.

## 1. Air temperature is not a complete heat balance

Human heat balance includes metabolic heat, work, convection, radiation, conduction and evaporation. Air cooler than skin can support convective heat loss; hotter air can instead supply heat. Radiant exchange also depends on surrounding surfaces and exposure. Convection and radiation do not both stop at a universal air-temperature threshold of 27°C or 30°C.

Wind affects heat and moisture transfer near the skin, but does not always provide the same cooling benefit. High humidity can hinder evaporation; relative humidity of 80–90% is not itself saturation. At a given air temperature, saturation corresponds to 100% relative humidity. Skin temperature and the vapour-pressure difference also matter.

## 2. Wind chill has a cold-weather domain

The [NWS wind-chill explanation](https://www.weather.gov/safety/cold-wind-chill-chart) describes an exposed-face heat-loss model without solar warming. It does not directly measure skin temperature or a garment's insulation. The Celsius and km/h form is:

$$T_{wc}=13.12+0.6215T-11.37V^{0.16}+0.3965TV^{0.16}$$

Here $T$ is air temperature in °C and $V$ is wind speed in km/h. Convert m/s before substitution: $V=3.6v$. NWS defines its index for temperatures at or below 50°F and wind speeds above 3mph: 10°C and approximately 4.8km/h, respectively. Do not extrapolate the equation into calm or warm conditions and label the result a precise physiological measurement.

### Worked example: −5°C and 5m/s

The wind speed is $5×3.6=18$km/h; $18^{0.16}≈1.588$. Substitution gives $T_{wc}≈-11.2$°C. This is a rounded model result, not an observation or a personal frostbite countdown. The air remains at −5°C; wind alone does not cool a dry inanimate object below the air temperature.

## 3. Heat index is not WBGT

The [NWS heat-tools explanation](https://www.weather.gov/safety/heat-index) describes heat index in terms of air temperature and relative humidity, with shade and light-wind assumptions. Do not transfer it unchanged to sunny activity or equate it with every Korean heat-warning criterion.

WBGT addresses a different set of environmental measurements, including natural wet-bulb and globe temperatures. Wind and radiant conditions matter. An ordinary air-temperature and humidity reading is not the same as measuring these quantities, and computing a wet-bulb temperature alone does not establish WBGT.

## 4. Read the label before the number

Identify the index, issuing institution, units and environmental assumptions. Check location, time and whether the value is a maximum or an average. Then consult the actual warning and action guidance. Do not attach one index's risk categories to another simply because both use degrees.

Twelve test subjects are not twelve clinical trials. The fitted exponent 0.16 does not by itself explain an individual's neural response. Nor does the index guarantee that a garment provides exactly 5°C of protection or that a particular person will develop frostbite after a fixed interval.

## Summary

Wind chill concerns cold and wind; heat index concerns heat and humidity; WBGT uses a different environmental heat-stress framework. Keep model outputs, observations and personal health assessments separate.
