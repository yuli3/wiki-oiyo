The Mu-In (戊寅) Ilju is the 15th pillar of the Sexagenary Cycle. It consists of the Heavenly Stem Mu (Yang Earth), representing an unwavering great mountain, sitting atop the Earthly Branch In (Tiger), representing a valiant tiger. It is the image of a great mountain where a tiger roars—a symbol of dignity and momentum.

## Personality and Temperament

The Mu-In Ilju is a "general" type who possesses both drive and a sense of responsibility. Because it sits in the position of *Jangsaeng* (Birth), its spirit remains unbroken, and it protects the organization it is entrusted with until the end.

- **Strengths**: Strong drive, charisma, and the sense of responsibility to carry an organization on one's shoulders.
- **Points of Caution**: One may take on too much alone and collapse from overwork.

## Structural Reading — Myeongri Interpretation

The [Mu (戊) Day Master](/en/meaning-of-saju-stem-mu/) sits atop the [In (寅) Earthly Branch](/en/meaning-of-saju-branch-in/). Based on the main qi of the Ilji, the 10 God is [Pyeongwan (偏官)](/en/meaning-of-saju-god-pyeongwan/), and the 12 Stages of Life is [Jangsaeng (長生)](/en/meaning-of-saju-stage-jangsaeng/). Pyeongwan is the star of challenge and responsibility—it creates a strong vessel that is forged by enduring pressure. As it is in the position of *Jangsaeng*, this structure is accompanied by new beginnings, learning, and the support of noble people (*Gwiin*).

In terms of Nabeum Five Elements, it belongs to *Seongduto* (Earth of the City Walls), a classification frequently used in traditional Myeongri to group pillars that share the same Nabeum.
