<HighlightBox type="abstract" title="Definition">
  The **Tiger** is the third animal in the Chinese zodiac and is paired with the
  Earthly Branch **Yin**. It is a symbolic cultural marker in the 12-year cycle,
  not a deterministic predictor of personality, luck, or destiny.
</HighlightBox>

## Cultural associations and traditional traits

The Tiger is widely associated with courage, force, protection, independence,
and visible presence. In East Asian folklore and art, tigers often appear as
powerful beings that ward off harm, confront danger, or represent untamed
energy. Zodiac language borrows from this symbolic background.

Popular descriptions may frame Tiger-year people as bold, competitive, direct,
or restless. These are traditional associations, not scientific personality
claims. A responsible definition treats the Tiger as a cultural image for
strength and movement, while recognizing that actual personality is shaped by
many biological, social, historical, and personal factors.

## Earthly Branch and cycle position

The Tiger corresponds to **Yin**, the third of the 12 Earthly Branches. It
follows Ox and precedes Rabbit in the standard zodiac sequence. The animal is
the public-facing label; the branch is the calendrical position. In a complete
stem-branch year, Yin is paired with one of the 10 Heavenly Stems.

Representative recent Tiger years include **1974, 1986, 1998, 2010, and
2022**. These are common Gregorian-year labels. People born in January or early
February should confirm the specific calendar boundary, because popular Lunar
New Year reckoning and Four Pillars solar-term reckoning can differ.

## Compatibility folklore

In compatibility folklore, the Tiger is often grouped with **Horse** and
**Dog** as a harmonious triad. Tiger and **Monkey** are commonly described as a
clashing pair. These patterns are traditional symbolic classifications. They
are not evidence that two people are inherently compatible or incompatible.

## Common Misunderstanding

The Tiger sign does not prove that someone is brave, aggressive, charismatic,
or destined for conflict. It is a birth-year animal attached to the Earthly
Branch Yin. A full East Asian fortune reading uses the Four Pillars, or saju,
including year, month, day, hour, stems, branches, elements, and interpretive
rules. See the broader [Chinese zodiac definition](/en/meaning-of-chinese-zodiac/)
and the related [Palja definition](/en/meaning-of-palja/) for context.

## Responsible use

Tiger symbolism is a cultural and symbolic tradition for reflection and
entertainment. It should not be used as deterministic prediction or as a basis
for judging personality, risk, relationships, or life outcomes.
