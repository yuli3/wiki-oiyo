The Byeong-In (丙寅) Ilju is the 3rd pillar of the sexagenary cycle. It represents the Heavenly Stem Byeong (Yang Fire), the sun that illuminates all things, sitting atop the Earthly Branch In, the brave tiger. It is the image of a rising force—the morning sun brightly illuminating the forest where the tiger dwells.

## Personality and Temperament

The Byeong-In Ilju is a natural optimist who is bright, easygoing, and a quick learner. They stand out wherever they go, receive affection from their superiors, and never hesitate to start new endeavors.

- **Strengths**: Innate cheerfulness, intuition, luck with noblemen (benefactors), and rapid learning ability.
- **Points of Caution**: They cool down as quickly as they heat up. They need to cultivate the habit of seeing things through to completion.

## Structural Analysis — Saju Interpretation

The [Byeong (丙) Day Master](/ko/meaning-of-saju-stem-byeong/) sits atop the [In (寅) Earthly Branch](/en/meaning-of-saju-branch-in/). Based on the main qi of the Ilji (daily branch), the Ten God is [Pyeonin (偏印)](/en/meaning-of-saju-god-pyeonin/), and the 12 Stages of Life is [Jangsaeng (長生)](/ko/meaning-of-saju-stage-jangsaeng/). Pyeonin is the star of intuition and specialized talent—unique ideas and expertise grow from the Ilji. Because it sits in the position of Jangsaeng, this structure is characterized by new beginnings, learning, and the support of noblemen.

In terms of Nabeum Five Elements, it belongs to Nojunghwa (爐中火, Fire in the Furnace), a classification frequently used in classical Saju studies to group pillars sharing the same Nabeum.
