When Korean news reports that "the Bank of Korea held the base rate," a string of stories on the stock market, loan rates and the exchange rate usually follows. This chapter explains what the base rate (기준금리, gijun geumni) is, and the path by which a single decision reaches a household's wallet.

---

## 1. What the base rate means

The base rate is the policy rate set by the **Monetary Policy Board (금융통화위원회, often shortened to 금통위)**, the Bank of Korea's highest decision-making body. It is the reference rate the Bank of Korea uses when it buys and sells repurchase agreements (RP) with financial institutions, or when it operates its standing facilities (대기성 여수신: liquidity adjustment deposits and loans).

- **Who decides**: the 7 members of the Monetary Policy Board (the Governor of the Bank of Korea chairs it)
- **How often**: meetings on the direction of monetary policy (통화정책방향 결정회의) are held **8 times a year**, roughly every 6 weeks.
- **What for**: the first objective set by the Bank of Korea Act is **price stability**. The Bank of Korea uses consumer price inflation of **2%** as its medium-term inflation target.

The base rate is not a rate that banks apply directly to their customers. It is an **operating target** that guides the rates on short-term funding between financial institutions, and other rates are set in the market using that target as a starting point.

---

## 2. How the base rate reaches your wallet

```
Monetary Policy Board decision (base rate)
  └─> Overnight rates (call rate, RP rate)
        └─> Short-term market rates (91-day CD, bank debentures)
              ├─> Variable-rate loans (benchmark such as COFIX + bank spread)
              ├─> Deposit and savings rates
              └─> Bond yields → stock valuations, won/dollar exchange rate
```

| Stage | What moves | How fast |
|---|---|---|
| Stage 1 | Call rate, RP rate | Day of the decision to a few days |
| Stage 2 | CD rate, bank debenture yields | A few days to a few weeks (the market sometimes prices it in before the decision) |
| Stage 3 | COFIX-linked variable-rate loans, deposit rates | A few weeks to a few months, following the COFIX publication cycle (once a month) |
| Stage 4 | Consumption, investment, prices | Usually several quarters or more |

The key point here is that **the market moves before the decision**. If bond market participants expect a cut, rates start falling before the decision, and on the announcement day the market only reacts to whether the outcome "was as expected." That is why the market reaction on the day depends less on the decision itself than on **the gap from expectations**.

---

## 3. What to read first in the announcement

On meeting days the Bank of Korea publishes its "Monetary Policy Decision" statement (「통화정책방향」) and the Governor holds a press conference. The following four things carry more information than the single number.

1. **The decision and the vote**: whether the rate was held, raised or cut, and whether there was a **dissenting opinion** (소수의견). Even with a hold, a dissent in favour of a cut is read as a signal of a cut at the next meeting.
2. **The inflation outlook sentence**: whether wording such as "inflation is expected to stabilise around the target level" has changed.
3. **The growth and financial stability sentences**: whether mentions of household debt, housing prices and exchange rate volatility have increased. The reasons it is hard to cut rates are written here.
4. **The forward-looking sentences**: at the press conference, how many members gave which three-month-ahead rate view (forward guidance).

<Callout type="important" title="Common misunderstandings">
- **A base rate cut means my loan rate falls by the same amount?** No. A loan rate is a benchmark rate (COFIX, bank debentures and so on) plus a spread set by the bank. If the spread rises, the loan rate falls by less even when the base rate is cut.
- **Fixed-rate loans change right away too?** Not during the fixed period. New fixed rates follow long-term bond yields more than the base rate.
</Callout>

## 4. A worked example

Suppose you borrowed 200 million won (2억 원) at a variable rate, and the rate fell by 0.25 percentage points, from 4.0% to 3.75% a year (a simple interest-only calculation with the principal unchanged).

- Annual interest before: 200 million won × 4.0% = **8 million won**
- Annual interest after: 200 million won × 3.75% = **7.5 million won**
- Difference: **500,000 won** a year, about **42,000 won** a month

"0.25 percentage points" looks small, but the larger the loan, the more it is felt. On the other side, for a deposit of 20 million won, the same 0.25 percentage points is a difference of 50,000 won a year (before tax).

---

## Summary

The base rate is the policy rate the Bank of Korea sets 8 times a year, and it spreads slowly in this order: overnight rates → market rates → loan and deposit rates → consumption and prices. On announcement day, read the dissenting opinions, the inflation and financial stability sentences, and the gap from market expectations before the number itself.
