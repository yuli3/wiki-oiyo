The Byeong-Sin (丙申) Ilju is the 33rd pillar of the sexagenary cycle. It represents the Heavenly Stem Byeong (丙), the Yang Fire of the sun that illuminates all things, sitting upon the Earthly Branch Sin (申), the talented monkey. It is the image of a great sun setting in the western sky—a traveler (Yeokma) that shines from afar and is constantly on the move.

## Personality and Temperament

The Byeong-Sin Ilju is a "traveler" type with a wide range of activity and excellent business sense. They gain energy from new places and new people, and they excel at expanding their horizons.

- **Strengths**: Broad range of activity, business acumen, and an open personality.
- **Points of Caution**: Their ability to initiate projects is stronger than their ability to finish them. A partner who specializes in wrapping things up is a great asset.

## Structural Interpretation — Myeongri Analysis

The [Byeong (丙) Day Master](/ko/meaning-of-saju-stem-byeong/) sits atop the [Sin (申) Earthly Branch](/en/meaning-of-saju-branch-sin-monkey/). Based on the primary energy (Jeonggi) of the Ilji, the Ten God is [Pyeonjae (偏財)](/en/meaning-of-saju-god-pyeonjae/), and the 12 Stages of Life (12 Unseong) is [Byeong (病)](/ko/meaning-of-saju-stage-byeong/). Pyeonjae is the star of fluid wealth, meaning the instinct for managing money and opportunities is embedded in the Ilji. Because it is in the position of Byeong, it is a structure where sensitivity and empathy are deepened.

In terms of Nabeum Five Elements, it belongs to Sanha-hwa (山下火), a classification frequently used in traditional Myeongri to group pillars that share the same Nabeum.
