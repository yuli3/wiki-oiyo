<HighlightBox type="abstract" title="Definition">
  The **Rabbit** is the fourth animal in the Chinese zodiac and is paired with
  the Earthly Branch **Mao**. It is a symbolic and cultural cycle marker, not a
  deterministic personality profile or fortune-telling result.
</HighlightBox>

## Cultural associations and traditional traits

The Rabbit is traditionally linked with gentleness, diplomacy, sensitivity,
care, and social grace. In popular zodiac language, it may suggest tact,
refinement, caution, or an ability to avoid unnecessary conflict. These
associations are cultural images rather than measured traits.

Regional traditions can vary. The Chinese and Korean lists usually use Rabbit,
while some Vietnamese traditions use Cat in the same position. That difference
reflects local history and language, not a mistake in the cycle. In the English
definition layer, Rabbit refers to the common Chinese-zodiac animal associated
with the branch Mao.

## Earthly Branch and cycle position

The Rabbit corresponds to **Mao**, the fourth of the 12 Earthly Branches. It
follows Tiger and precedes Dragon. The animal name is the familiar mnemonic,
while Mao is the underlying branch used in the traditional calendar system.
With Heavenly Stems, the branch participates in the 60-year cycle.

Representative recent Rabbit years include **1975, 1987, 1999, 2011, and
2023**. These are common Gregorian-year labels. Births near January or early
February require caution, because the zodiac year may be counted from Lunar New
Year in popular usage or from a solar-term boundary in Four Pillars practice.

## Compatibility folklore

Compatibility folklore often groups the Rabbit with **Goat** and **Pig** in a
harmonious triad. Rabbit and **Rooster** are commonly described as a clashing
pair. These pairings are symbolic traditions, not reliable evidence about
whether two people will understand, love, or support each other.

## Common Misunderstanding

The Rabbit sign does not determine that a person is gentle, artistic,
conflict-avoidant, lucky, or fragile. It is a broad birth-year label. Complete
East Asian fortune reading uses the Four Pillars, or saju, which considers the
year, month, day, and hour of birth through stems and branches. See the broader
[Chinese zodiac definition](/en/meaning-of-chinese-zodiac/) and the related
[Palja definition](/en/meaning-of-palja/) for the full context.

## Responsible use

Rabbit symbolism belongs to cultural tradition, reflection, and entertainment.
It should not be used as deterministic prediction, social sorting, or proof of
personality or destiny.
