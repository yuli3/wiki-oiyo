Pyeonjae (偏財) represents "moving money" rather than a fixed salary—it is business income, investment returns, and wealth that flows through opportunities. It is the mindset of a "big player" who views money as something to be managed rather than merely possessed.

## At a Glance

| Category | Description |
|---|---|
| Definition | The element I control (剋) that shares the same Yin-Yang polarity |
| Governed Areas | Fluid wealth, business, social skills, spatial awareness |
| Key Keywords | Business acumen, large sums of money, sociability, activity |

## Personality Manifestations

Those with a prominent Pyeonjae are generous with their spending, have a wide social circle, and possess a keen "nose" for money (opportunities). They are quick with spatial reasoning and mental calculations, and they often invest in people to create larger opportunities.

## In Work and Career

Their abilities shine in environments where money circulates on a large scale, such as business, sales, investment, distribution, and real estate.

## In Wealth

Money comes in large amounts, but it also goes out in large amounts. The principle of "never touching the seed money" is what determines the success or failure of Pyeonjae.

## When Excessive or Deficient

When excessive, one may be swayed by the desire for "get-rich-quick" schemes, increasing the risks of romantic entanglements and overspending. It is a long-standing maxim in Saju (명리) that one must have a strong Day Master (신강) to handle great wealth.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. If I have Pyeonjae, should I start a business?**

A. Pyeonjae represents the "potential for business acumen," not a mandate to start a company. For office workers, this energy can be sufficiently expressed through variable income streams such as performance bonuses, investments, or side hustles. However, betting one's entire fortune is the worst way to utilize Pyeonjae.

*Sipsin is a language for interpreting the relationships between the Day Master (the self) and other characters. The manifestation of the same Sipsin varies depending on its position, strength, and the presence of combinations (合) or clashes (沖); there is no such thing as an inherently "good" or "bad" Sipsin.*
