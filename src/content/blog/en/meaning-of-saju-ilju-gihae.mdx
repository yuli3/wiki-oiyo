The Gi-Hae (己亥) Ilju is the 36th pillar of the sexagenary cycle. It consists of the Heavenly Stem Gi (Yin Earth), representing the soil that nurtures all things, sitting atop the Earthly Branch Hae (the Pig), representing abundance. It resembles fertile reclaimed land by the sea—a form that accepts vast waters to become rich, arable soil.

## Personality and Temperament

The Gi-Hae Ilju is a virtuous, leader-like type with great capacity for inclusivity and a natural affinity for wealth. They are adept at embracing diverse people and possess the talent to transform what they absorb into tangible results.

- **Strengths**: Generous inclusivity, flexible negotiation skills, and financial luck.
- **Points of Caution**: They can be easily swayed by others' opinions; maintaining a firm sense of conviction is their "levee" for protecting their fertile soil.

## Structural Analysis — Myeongri Interpretation

The [Gi (己) Day Master](/ko/meaning-of-saju-stem-gi/) sits atop the [Hae (亥) Earthly Branch](/en/meaning-of-saju-branch-hae/). Based on the main Qi of the branch, the 10 Gods correspond to [Jeongjae (正財 - Direct Wealth)](/en/meaning-of-saju-god-jeongjae/), and the 12 Stages of Life correspond to [Tae (胎 - Conception)](/ko/meaning-of-saju-stage-tae/). As the star of fixed wealth, Jeongjae provides a foundation oriented toward stability, diligent accumulation, and management. The position of "Tae" (Conception) suggests a structure that is always harboring new possibilities and ideas.

In terms of Nabeum (phonetic elements), it belongs to Pyeongji-mok (平地木), a classification frequently used in traditional Myeongri alongside other pillars sharing the same Nabeum.
