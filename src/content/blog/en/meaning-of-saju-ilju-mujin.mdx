The Mujin (戊辰) Ilju is the 5th pillar of the sexagenary cycle. It represents the Heavenly Stem Mu (Yang Earth), a steady, immovable mountain, sitting atop the Earthly Branch Jin (the Dragon), which embodies change. It is an image of a great mountain harboring a dragon—a combination of gravitas and ambition.

## Personality and Temperament

The Mujin Ilju is a "great person" type who possesses a broad perspective and values integrity. They have a distinct sense of self, are rarely swayed, and often take on the role of a pillar within an organization.

- **Strengths**: A sense of profound reliability, the ambition to envision the "big picture," and perseverance in times of crisis.
- **Points of Caution**: They may lack flexibility and possess a strong sense of pride, making it difficult for them to know when to yield.

## Structural Analysis — Myeongri Interpretation

The [Mu (戊) Day Master](/en/meaning-of-saju-stem-mu/) sits upon the [Jin (辰) Earthly Branch](/en/meaning-of-saju-branch-jin/). Based on the main energy of the Ilji (Day Branch), the 10 God is [Bigyeon (比肩)](/en/meaning-of-saju-god-bigyeon/), and the 12 Stages of Life is [Gwandae (冠帶)](/en/meaning-of-saju-stage-gwandae/). Bigyeon is the star of the ego and subjectivity—the power to stand on one's own is supported by the Ilji. As it sits in the Gwandae position, it is a structure of upward mobility, characterized by the spirit to step onto the stage with vigor.

In terms of Nabeum, it belongs to Daerimmok (Great Forest Wood), a classification often used in traditional Myeongri to group pillars sharing the same Nabeum element.
