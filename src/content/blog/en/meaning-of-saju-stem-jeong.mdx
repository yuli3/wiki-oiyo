Jeong (丁) is a candle lit in the dark. It may not illuminate the whole world like the sun, but it brightens exactly where it is needed, exactly as much as needed. A small flame that does not go out — the passion of Jeong fire burns inwardly.

## Personality and Temperament

A Jeong fire day master is warm and delicate, concentrating deeply on one field or one person. Even if they seem calm on the outside, something hot burns within, and they spare no devotion for what they cherish. Courtesy and consideration are deeply ingrained in them.

- **Strengths**: Focus and persistence, plus the warmth to care for others. A presence that shines even more in darkness, or crisis.
- **Points to Watch**: They are sensitive and do not easily reveal what is inside, which can lead to misunderstandings. They tend to store up hurt feelings and then release them all at once, and they are deeply wounded when their devotion is not reciprocated.

## Suitable Fields

Research and technology, medicine, counseling, religion and spiritual fields, precision crafts, and other work that requires depth and sincerity.

## Relationships with Other Characters

When Jeong meets Gap wood (firewood), the flame lasts a long time; when it meets Gyeong metal (cast iron), it refines it through smelting, or alchemy, to make a great vessel — this is Jeong fire at its highest use.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Which is better, Jeong fire or Byeong fire?**

A. It is not a matter of superiority, but a difference in purpose. Byeong fire is the light that nurtures all things, while Jeong fire is the fire that melts metal to make tools. It is most accurate to understand this as the difference between a stage-oriented temperament and an artisan temperament.
