The Jeong-Myo (丁卯) Ilju is the 4th pillar of the Sexagenary Cycle. It represents the Heavenly Stem Jeong (Yin Fire), a candlelight illuminating the darkness, sitting upon the Earthly Branch Myo, a gentle, growing rabbit. It is the image of a candle illuminating a field of clover on a spring night—a figure of delicate and refined sensibility.

## Personality and Temperament

The Jeong-Myo Ilju is an artistic type with rich sensitivity and a highly developed aesthetic sense. They are affectionate and deeply thoughtful, but this also makes them prone to being easily hurt.

- **Strengths**: Delicate sensibility, aesthetic appreciation, and devoted kindness.
- **Points of Caution**: If their sensitivity becomes excessive, they may dwell on trivial remarks for a long time. They require outlets for emotional release.

## Structural Interpretation — Myeongri Analysis

The [Jeong (丁) Day Master](/en/meaning-of-saju-stem-jeong/) sits upon the [Myo (卯) Branch](/en/meaning-of-saju-branch-myo/). Based on the main Qi of the daily branch, the 10 God is the [Pyeonin (Indirect Seal)](/en/meaning-of-saju-god-pyeonin/), and the 12 Stages of Life is [Byeong (Sickness)](/en/meaning-of-saju-stage-byeong/). The Pyeonin is the star of intuition and specialized talent—extraordinary ideas and expertise grow from the daily branch. Because it sits in the stage of 'Sickness' (Byeong), it is a structure characterized by deep sensitivity and empathy.

In terms of Nabeum Ohaeng, it belongs to Nojunghwa (爐中火, Fire in the Furnace), a classification frequently used in traditional Myeongri to group pillars sharing the same Nabeum.
