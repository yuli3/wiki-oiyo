# Codependency: A Relationship Pattern of Losing Yourself in Another Person's Problems

**Codependency** refers to a relationship pattern in which a person becomes so excessively absorbed in caring for and controlling another person's emotions, problems, or addiction that they lose sight of their own needs and identity. The concept originally emerged from descriptions of spouses of people with alcohol addiction in the recovery movement of the 1980s, and today it is used more broadly to explain imbalance in many kinds of close relationships.

## Key Features

- **Excessive responsibility**: Feeling that one must take responsibility for the other person's emotions and outcomes.
- **Blurred boundaries**: The needs of "me" and "you" are not clearly separated, and saying no is experienced as guilt.
- **Externalized self-worth**: Confirming one's value only through approval and being needed.
- **Control and rescue**: Trying to "fix" the other person, becoming depleted in the process.

## Academic Position

Codependency is not an official diagnosis in the DSM-5; it is a **clinical and popular psychology concept**. Measurement tools exist, such as the Spann-Fischer Codependency Scale, but because there is debate over construct validity, it is best treated not as a fixed identity but as **language for examining relationship dynamics**.

## Direction of Recovery

Recovery does not mean "stopping care." It means establishing **healthy boundaries**. The starting point is to first recognize one's own feelings and needs, distinguish what belongs to the other person from what belongs to oneself, and practice saying no without guilt.
