The Gi-Myo (己卯) pillar is the 16th in the Sexagenary Cycle. It consists of the Heavenly Stem Gi (Yin Earth), representing the soil that nurtures all things, sitting upon the Earthly Branch Myo (Rabbit), representing gentle growth. It is often visualized as a rabbit grazing in a field after a spring rain—a gentle yet industrious image.

## Personality and Temperament

Though those with the Gi-Myo pillar may appear gentle, they are of the "gentle exterior, inner strength" (外柔內剛) type, firmly protecting their own territory. They manage their responsibilities with meticulous care, leaving no gaps.

- **Strengths**: Meticulous management skills, diligence, and smooth interpersonal skills.
- **Points of Caution**: They tend to accept things outwardly while harboring stress inwardly. A healthy outlet for stress is essential.

## Structural Analysis — Myeongri Interpretation

The [Gi (己) Day Master](/ko/meaning-of-saju-stem-gi/) sits upon the [Myo (卯) Branch](/en/meaning-of-saju-branch-myo/). Based on the main Qi of the Day Branch, the Ten God is [Pyeongwan (偏官)](/en/meaning-of-saju-god-pyeongwan/), and the Twelve Stages of Life is [Byeong (病)](/ko/meaning-of-saju-stage-byeong/). Pyeongwan is the star of challenge and responsibility—it creates a vessel that is tempered by enduring pressure. Being in the stage of 'Sickness' (Byeong), the structure is characterized by deep sensitivity and empathy.

In terms of Nabeum, it belongs to Seongduto (城頭土, Earth of the City Wall), a classification frequently used in traditional Myeongri alongside other pillars sharing the same Nabeum.
