The Gye-Chuk (癸丑) Ilju is the 50th pillar of the sexagenary cycle. It represents the Heavenly Stem Gye (癸), a Yin-Water symbolizing moist, permeating rainwater, sitting upon the Earthly Branch Chuk (丑), the Ox, which signifies silent endurance. It is the image of a spring bubbling up from frozen mud in the dead of winter—a symbol of unceasing willpower.

## Personality and Temperament

The Gye-Chuk Ilju is a resolute type characterized by deep patience and a strong sense of responsibility. Despite a gentle exterior, the inner self is as cold and firm as a winter spring.

- **Strengths**: Strong sense of responsibility, resolve to endure adversity.
- **Points of Caution**: Prone to accumulating suppressed frustration (Hwabyung). Even a spring beneath the ice needs a path to flow.

## Structural Analysis — Myeongri Interpretation

The [Gye (癸) Day Master](/en/meaning-of-saju-stem-gye/) sits upon the [Chuk (丑) Earthly Branch](/en/meaning-of-saju-branch-chuk/). Based on the main Qi of the Ilji (daily branch), the 10 God is [Pyeongwan (偏官)](/en/meaning-of-saju-god-pyeongwan/), and the 12 Stages of Life is [Gwandae (冠帶)](/en/meaning-of-saju-stage-gwandae/). Pyeongwan is the star of challenge and responsibility—it creates a strong vessel that is forged by enduring pressure. As it sits in the position of Gwandae, it represents a rising structure that steps onto the stage with ambition.

In terms of Nabeum Five Elements, it belongs to Sangja-mok (桑柘木). This classification was frequently used in traditional Myeongri, grouping it with other pillars sharing the same Nabeum.
