<HighlightBox type="abstract" title="Definition">
  The **life line** is the major line that usually curves around the base of the
  thumb and the mount below it. In palmistry, it is traditionally read as a
  symbolic line of vitality, grounding, stamina, and life rhythm. It does
  **not** predict lifespan, diagnose health, or determine a fixed fate.
</HighlightBox>

## What It Is / Location

The life line usually begins near the space between the thumb and index finger,
then arcs downward around the thumb base toward the wrist. Readers often trace
its curve around the fleshy area below the thumb, sometimes called the Mount of
Venus in Western palmistry.

Because the name sounds dramatic, this line is often misunderstood. In careful
definition work, the life line is not a clock, a death marker, or a medical
chart. Its length, depth, branches, and breaks belong to palmistry's symbolic
vocabulary, not to evidence-based health prediction.

## Traditional Interpretations

The life line is traditionally read as a symbol of vitality and grounding. A
clear, broad curve may be described in folklore as steady energy, physical
presence, or strong rootedness in daily life. A line that stays close to the
thumb may be read as more reserved or self-contained. Branches may be discussed
as symbols of movement, travel, changing routines, or shifting life focus.

Some readers also compare the life line with the head line at the starting
point. A joined beginning may be interpreted as cautious or family-connected,
while a separated beginning may be read as independent. These interpretations
are traditional and conversational, not factual claims about the body.

## Common Misunderstanding

The most important correction is simple: a short life line does **not** mean a
short life. A broken life line does not mean a fatal event. A faint life line
does not mean illness, weakness, infertility, disability, or poor health.

Palm lines can be affected by ordinary hand shape, skin texture, work patterns,
age, scars, and natural variation. If someone has a real concern about pain,
skin changes, circulation, injury, or other physical symptoms, the appropriate
response is medical care, not palm reading.

Palmistry is for entertainment, self-reflection, and cultural learning. Palm
lines do **not** diagnose health, disease, or medical conditions, and palmistry
is not medical, financial, or legal advice. For the broader context, see
[Palmistry](/en/meaning-of-palmistry/).
