The Sinmi (辛未) pillar is the 8th in the sexagenary cycle, where the Heavenly Stem Sin (Yin Metal), representing a sharply shining jewel, sits upon the Earthly Branch Mi (Yang Earth), representing devoted soil. It is the image of a jewel buried in dry summer earth—a symbol of sharpness waiting for its time.

## Personality and Temperament

The Sinmi pillar represents a "soft on the outside, firm on the inside" personality; they appear gentle but possess a mind as sharp as a blade. They pursue perfection in their professional fields and rarely reveal their true inner thoughts.

- **Strengths**: Professional sharpness, silent endurance, and impeccable self-discipline.
- **Points of Caution**: Because their standards are high, they can become demanding toward both themselves and others.

## Reading the Structure — Saju Interpretation

The [Sin (辛) Day Master](/ko/meaning-of-saju-stem-sin/) sits upon the [Mi (未) Branch](/en/meaning-of-saju-branch-mi/). Based on the main Qi of the branch, the 10 Gods correspond to [Pyeonin (Indirect Resource)](/en/meaning-of-saju-god-pyeonin/), and the 12 Stages of Life correspond to [Soe (Decline/Maturity)](/ko/meaning-of-saju-stage-soe/). Pyeonin is the star of intuition and specialized talent—unique ideas and expertise grow from this pillar. As it sits in the stage of Soe, it is a structure that prioritizes seasoned substance over outward flashiness.

In terms of Nabeum, it belongs to Nobangto (Roadside Earth), a classification frequently used in traditional Saju studies to group pillars sharing the same phonetic element.
