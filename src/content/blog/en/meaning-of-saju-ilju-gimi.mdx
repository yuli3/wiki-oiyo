The Gimi (己未) pillar is the 56th in the sexagenary cycle. It consists of the Heavenly Stem Gi (Yin Earth), representing the soil that nurtures all things, sitting upon the Earthly Branch Mi (Yang Earth), which represents dedication. It is the image of a well-tended vegetable garden in midsummer—a small but complete kingdom of one's own.

## Personality and Temperament

The Gimi pillar represents a self-made individual who is practical and determined. They do not entrust the work within their domain to others, preferring to cultivate it themselves.

- **Strengths**: Solid practicality and the determination to protect one's own territory.
- **Points of Caution**: You may become so focused on your own garden that you miss the larger field. Try looking over the fence once in a while.

## Structural Analysis — Myeongri Interpretation

With the [Gi (己) Day Master](/ko/meaning-of-saju-stem-gi/) sitting on the [Mi (未) Branch](/en/meaning-of-saju-branch-mi/), the 10 Gods based on the main Qi of the daily branch is [Bigyeon (比肩)](/en/meaning-of-saju-god-bigyeon/), and the 12 Stages of Life is [Gwandae (冠帶)](/ko/meaning-of-saju-stage-gwandae/). Bigyeon is the star of the self and subjectivity—the power to stand on one's own is supported by the daily branch. As it sits in the position of Gwandae (the stage of wearing the official cap and belt), it is a structure of upward mobility and ambitious entry onto the stage.

In terms of Nabeum (phonetic elements), it belongs to Cheonsanghwa (天上火, Heavenly Fire), a classification often used in traditional Myeongri alongside other pillars sharing the same Nabeum.
