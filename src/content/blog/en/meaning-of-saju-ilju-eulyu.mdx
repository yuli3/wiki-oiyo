The Eul-Yu (乙酉) pillar is the 22nd in the sexagenary cycle, where the Heavenly Stem Eul (Yin Wood), representing a flexible, winding vine, sits upon the Earthly Branch Yu (the Rooster), representing precision. It is often likened to a wildflower blooming in a crevice of a rock—delicate in appearance, yet unbreakable.

## Personality and Temperament

Despite a delicate outward appearance, the Eul-Yu pillar is a practical type with strong survival instincts. They possess an excellent aesthetic sense and are capable of carving out a place for themselves in any environment.

- **Strengths**: Strong adaptability, exceptional aesthetic sensibility.
- **Points to Note**: Because their nerves are sensitive, they are prone to suffering in silence. Developing a sense of "insensitivity" (resilience to stress) is beneficial for their well-being.

## Structural Analysis — Saju Interpretation

The [Eul (乙) Day Master](/ko/meaning-of-saju-stem-eul/) sits upon the [Yu (酉) Earthly Branch](/en/meaning-of-saju-branch-yu/). Based on the main Qi of the Day Branch, the 10 Gods correspond to [Pyeongwan (偏官)](/en/meaning-of-saju-god-pyeongwan/), and the 12 Stages of Life correspond to [Jeol (絶)](/ko/meaning-of-saju-stage-jeol/). Pyeongwan is the star of challenge and responsibility—it creates a vessel that is forged through enduring pressure. The position of Jeol indicates a dynamic structure characterized by repeated cycles of change and resurgence.

In terms of Nabeum (phonetic elements), it belongs to Cheonjungsu (泉中水), a classification frequently used in traditional Saju analysis to group pillars sharing the same Nabeum.
