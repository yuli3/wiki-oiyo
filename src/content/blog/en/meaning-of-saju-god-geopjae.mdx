As its name—which literally means "plundering wealth"—suggests, Geopjae (劫財) is another version of the self that shares the same energy but is subtly misaligned due to a different Yin-Yang polarity. It represents a powerful drive for competition, a large-scale ambition, and a variable factor in the fluctuations of one's wealth.

## Personality Manifestation

Individuals with a prominent Geopjae tend to be magnanimous and bold when faced with a challenge. Rather than seeking ordinary stability, they aim for significant leaps and exert strength beyond their usual capacity in competitive situations. They also possess a natural charisma that attracts others.

## In Career and Work

They demonstrate their true value in highly competitive fields, such as sales, sports, investments, and projects where the outcome is decided by a win-or-lose scenario.

## In Wealth

Their financial structure is prone to "earning big and spending big." Implementing a system that automatically isolates a portion of their income (such as savings or pensions) is essentially mandatory.

## When Excessive vs. Deficient

When excessive, it is easy for wealth to leak due to reckless betting, acting as a guarantor, or issues arising from business partnerships, and it can create unnecessary competitive friction with those around them. When balanced, it serves as a powerful weapon for breaking through crises.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Is Geopjae always a "bad" Sipsin?**

A. No. Geopjae is like a "beast-like drive"; if kept in a cage, it causes accidents, but if released into the hunting grounds, it becomes your greatest asset. In a weak Saju chart (Sinyak), it can even become a reliable ally that supports you. The key is to provide the Geopjae with a stage (competition or challenge) where it can work effectively.

---

*Sipsin is a language used to interpret the relationship between other characters based on the Ilgan (the self). The manifestation of the same Sipsin varies depending on its position, strength, and interactions (Hap/Chung); there is no such thing as an inherently "good" or "bad" Sipsin.*
