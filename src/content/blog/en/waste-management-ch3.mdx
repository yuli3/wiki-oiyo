South Korea recycles approximately 98% of its collected municipal food waste into dried animal feed, rich organic compost, and anaerobic biomethane gas. Because nearly all collected organic mass enters secondary livestock feed processing lines, the statutory standard separating food waste from regular combustible waste is straightforward: **"Is this material suitable and digestible for animal consumption?"**

Anything that cannot be pulverized by commercial shredder blades, contains excessive mineral salts, or carries toxic substances must be diverted away from food bins into regular volume-based combustible bags.

Exact item lists differ by city or district in South Korea, so check your local government's guide as well.

According to Ministry of Environment public standards and the Control of Livestock and Fish Feed Act, the following culinary residuals must be routed to general combustible bags:

### Hard Shells, Bones, and Exoskeletons
- **Mammal and Poultry Bones**: Hard beef femur bones, pork ribs, and chicken bones cause catastrophic torque overloads and blade fractures in commercial crushing grinders.
- **Seafood Exoskeletons**: Clam shells, oyster bivalves, abalone shells, crab legs, lobster carapaces, and crustacean claws consist of calcium carbonate and chitin that do not break down in livestock feed digestors.
- **Eggshells and Nutshells**: Walnut shells, chestnut casings, peanut hulls, and hard eggshells lack nutritional value for feed formulations.

### Toxic Pits and Fruit Seeds
- **Large Hard Fruit Pits**: Peach, plum, apricot, and persimmon stones contain trace cyanogenic glycosides and can shatter processing machinery.

### High-Fiber Peels and Vegetative Residuals
- **Fibrous Outer Skins**: Garlic skins, dry onion peels, and dry corn husks contain high levels of lignin that resist enzymatic microbial digestion and tangle rotary augers.
- **Root Whiskers**: Dried scallion and leek roots trap heavy grit that contaminates organic compost grades.

## 2. Industrial Processing: Rendering, Composting, and Biogas

Collected food waste undergoes three main industrial pathways depending on municipal contracts:

```
[Food Waste Inflow] ──> [Crushing & Desalination] ──┬──> [Thermal Drying ──> Animal Feed Pellet]
                                                   ├──> [Aerobic Fermentation ──> Organic Compost]
                                                   └──> [Anaerobic Digestion ──> Biomethane Fuel]
```

1. **Animal Feed Formulation**: Slurry is desalted, heated to over 100°C for sterilization, dehydrated, and pelleted into shelf-stable swine and poultry feed.
2. **Aerobic Fermentation**: Moisture is regulated with sawdust or wood shavings, and microbial digestion generates nutrient-dense horticultural soil conditioners.
3. **Anaerobic Biomethane Digestion**: Sludge decomposes in oxygen-free digester tanks, capturing methane ($CH_4$) gas to power municipal fleets or generate clean electricity.
