<HighlightBox type="abstract" title="Definition">
  **Palmistry**, also called **palm reading** or **chiromancy**, is the
  interpretive practice of reading the hand's lines, mounts, shapes, fingers,
  and texture as symbolic clues about temperament and life themes. It is a
  cultural divination tradition, not a medical, scientific, or diagnostic
  method.
</HighlightBox>

## What Palmistry Is

Palmistry reads the hand as a symbolic map. A palm reader may look at the major
lines, minor lines, palm shape, finger proportions, skin texture, flexibility,
and raised areas called mounts. The reading then turns those observations into
an interpretive conversation about habits, strengths, worries, relationships,
motivation, or decision-making style.

Different schools disagree about details. Some traditions emphasize fixed line
meanings, while others treat the hand as a flexible mirror of lived experience.
OIYO defines palmistry as a symbolic and cultural practice, not as evidence of a
fixed future.

## Main Lines

Many modern palmistry systems begin with four major lines.

| Feature | Common Location | Traditional Reading Theme |
|---|---|---|
| Heart line | Upper palm below the fingers | emotion, attachment style, affection |
| Head line | Middle palm | thinking style, attention, judgment |
| Life line | Curves around the base of the thumb | vitality, grounding, life rhythm |
| Fate line | Vertical line toward the middle finger, when visible | vocation, direction, outside pressure |

The names can be misleading. In responsible palmistry, the **life line** is not
used to predict the length of someone's life. A short, broken, or faint line is
not a health diagnosis.

## Mounts, Shapes, and Other Features

Palmistry also uses raised areas of the palm called **mounts**, usually named
after planets in Western chiromancy. For example, the mount below the index
finger is often called Jupiter, while the mount below the thumb is often linked
with Venus.

Hand shape is another interpretive layer. Some systems group hands by elemental
types such as earth, air, fire, and water. Others focus on practical features:
whether fingers are long or short, whether the palm is square or rectangular,
and whether the hand feels firm or flexible.

These categories are symbolic. They should not be confused with anatomy,
psychology, or clinical assessment.

## History in Brief

Hand reading has appeared in both Eastern and Western traditions. Indian
samudrika traditions, Chinese face-and-hand reading, Greco-Roman references,
Arabic transmission, and later European occult writing all contributed to the
large family of practices now called palmistry or chiromancy.

In Europe, chiromancy was discussed in medieval and early modern texts, often
alongside astrology, physiognomy, and other interpretive arts. In the 19th and
early 20th centuries, popular palmistry manuals helped standardize the familiar
language of heart line, head line, life line, fate line, and planetary mounts.

## Common Misunderstanding

The most serious misunderstanding is that palm lines can diagnose disease,
predict death, or reveal medical facts. They cannot. Palmistry is not a health
screening method, and changes in the hand should be handled through appropriate
medical care when they raise real-world concern.

Another misunderstanding is that every mark has one fixed meaning. Responsible
readers usually interpret features in context: the whole hand, the person's
question, cultural tradition, and the limits of symbolic language.

## Responsible Use

Palmistry is for entertainment, self-reflection, journaling, cultural learning,
and symbolic conversation. Palm lines do **not** diagnose health, disease,
medical conditions, fertility, lifespan, mental illness, or disability.

Palmistry is not medical, financial, or legal advice. It is not a substitute for
qualified professionals, evidence-based care, emergency support, or informed
decision-making.

Use palmistry as a reflective prompt, not as a diagnosis or command.

### FAQ

**Which hand should be read?**  
Traditions differ. Some readers compare both hands, treating one as inherited
potential and the other as lived expression. This is symbolic convention, not a
scientific rule.

**Can palm lines change?**  
Small features of the hand can change with age, work, injury, skin condition,
and ordinary use. Palmistry may treat such changes symbolically, but medical
concerns should be taken to a qualified clinician.

**Does a short life line mean a short life?**  
No. The life line should not be used to predict lifespan or health outcomes.
