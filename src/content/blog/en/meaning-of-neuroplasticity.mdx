## Definition

Neuroplasticity is the brain and nervous system’s capacity to **adjust connections and response patterns through experience**.

It is one reason people can learn new skills, form habits, and soften old reactions through repeated practice.

## Core Structure

| Element | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Repetition | Repeated actions make related pathways easier to activate. |
| Attention | What you attend to shapes the direction of learning. |
| Reward | Small relief or success makes a behavior more likely to return. |
| Environment | Cues that make action easier help reinforce the pattern. |

## Relationship to Habit Formation

Habits are not built by willpower alone. When time, place, cue, and reward repeat together, the brain begins to treat the behavior as a more familiar option.

This is why small, repeated actions often work better than dramatic resolutions.

## Careful Use

Neuroplasticity does not mean everything can be changed instantly or by mindset alone. Sleep, stress, health, social support, and environment also matter.

When change feels slow, it is often more useful to make the condition smaller and more repeatable than to blame willpower.
