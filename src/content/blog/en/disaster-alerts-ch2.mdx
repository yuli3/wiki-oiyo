Civil defense warnings (민방위 경보, minbangwi gyeongbo) reach you through sirens and broadcasts before any text message. People in South Korea hear the siren every year during the air raid civil defense drill, but few can tell right away which warning it is. This chapter covers what you hear for each type of warning and what to do first.

---

## 1. Types of warning and their signals

How each warning is signaled is set by [Annex 1] Civil Defense Warning Signal Methods (민방위경보 신호방법) of the Enforcement Rule of the Framework Act on Civil Defense (민방위기본법 시행규칙). The current table separates warnings that sound a siren from warnings given only by voice broadcast.

| Warning | When it is issued | What you hear |
|---|---|---|
| **Alert warning** (경계경보, gyeonggye gyeongbo) | When an enemy attack is expected | **Voice broadcast** (radio, TV, disaster text alerts, indoor and outdoor broadcasts) |
| **Air raid warning** (공습경보, gongseup gyeongbo) | When an attack is imminent or under way | **1-minute wailing siren** + voice broadcast |
| **CBR warning** (화생방경보, hwasaengbang gyeongbo) | When chemical, biological or radiological agents have been released or detected | 1-minute wailing siren + voice broadcast |
| **Nuclear warning** (핵경보, haek gyeongbo) | A nuclear attack situation | 1-minute wailing siren + voice broadcast |
| **All clear** (경보해제, gyeongbo haeje) | When signs of attack have gone | **Voice broadcast** |

<Callout type="important" title="The alert-warning siren is a thing of the past">
Many older guides describe the alert warning as "a 1-minute steady tone." Under the current annex, the alert warning is given by voice broadcast. Because old posters may differ, treat any siren as **one of the air raid, CBR or nuclear warnings** and listen to the broadcast.
</Callout>

## 2. What the wailing tone sounds like

A wailing tone (파상음, pasangeum) is a siren that rises and falls over and over.

- **It rises for 5 seconds and falls for 3 seconds.** One cycle is 8 seconds.
- This repeats **7 times**, sounding for about 1 minute.

The disaster warning siren used for natural disasters has a different rhythm. According to a note in the same annex, it is a 12-second wailing tone that rises for 2 seconds and falls for 2 seconds, repeated three times. If you cannot tell from the sound alone, check which warning it is on radio, TV or a disaster text alert.

## 3. What to do first when you hear one

- **Air raid warning**: Go straight to the nearest **underground shelter** (a subway station, an underground shopping arcade, a building's underground parking garage, etc.). If you are driving, pull over to the right side of the road and take shelter.
- **CBR or nuclear warning**: Follow the evacuation direction given in the broadcast. Cover your nose and mouth with a mask or a wet cloth, and if you are indoors, close the windows and doors.
- **Alert warning**: Get ready to evacuate and keep listening to the broadcast.
- **All clear**: Return to normal life as the broadcast directs, but after a CBR warning, wait for guidance about contaminated areas.

Detailed actions differ by situation, so also check the emergency public action guidelines on the [National Disaster and Safety Portal (국민재난안전포털)](https://www.safekorea.go.kr).

## 4. Sirens you hear during drills

Every year the government holds an **air raid civil defense drill**, for example during the Ulchi exercise period, and sounds real sirens. The Ministry of the Interior and Safety and local governments announce the date and time in advance each year. When you hear a drill siren, it is a good chance to check where your nearest shelter is. How to find a shelter is covered in the next chapter.

</Callout>

## Summary

- The warnings that sound a siren (a 1-minute wailing tone) are the **air raid, CBR and nuclear warnings**.
- **The alert warning and the all clear are given by voice broadcast.**
- The wailing tone rises for 5 seconds and falls for 3 seconds, repeated 7 times.
- When you hear an air raid warning, go to the nearest underground shelter.
