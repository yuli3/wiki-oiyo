**A weather alert is not an automatic switch triggered by one observed number.** Read the phenomenon, quantity, duration, region and logical conditions together. This chapter concerns South Korea; its thresholds are not rules for other countries.

Criteria checked: **2026-10-19**, against the [KMA's current alert-criteria page](https://www.weather.go.kr/w/forecast/guide/standard.do). When criteria change, consult the official table and actual bulletin rather than treating this chapter as a live alert service.

## 1. Check the tiers for each phenomenon

Advisories and warnings are common categories, but not every phenomenon has only two tiers. The current table includes a heat **중대경보**, rendered here as a severe heat warning, and separate tropical-night advisory criteria. The dust row, by contrast, has no advisory criterion.

A warning does not mean damage must already have occurred. Many criteria concern what is expected. Observations inform assessment, but an observation, forecast and issued bulletin are different things.

## 2. Heavy rain and snow: keep the accumulation window

<section aria-label="Heavy-rain and heavy-snow criteria" className="my-6 grid gap-3">

<div className="rounded-lg border border-border bg-card p-4">

### Heavy rain

- Advisory: expected accumulation of **60mm or more in 3 hours**, **or 110mm or more in 12 hours**.
- Warning: **90mm or more in 3 hours**, **or 180mm or more in 12 hours**.
- Both windows need not be satisfied. These are accumulated depths, not rates expressed in mm/h.

</div>

<div className="rounded-lg border border-border bg-card p-4">

### Heavy snow

- Advisory: **5cm or more** expected to fall and accumulate within **24 hours**.
- Warning: **20cm or more** over that period, except **30cm or more in mountainous areas**.
- The mountain exception belongs to the warning criterion, not an invented 20cm advisory criterion. Fresh accumulation is not the total old snow already on the ground.

</div>

</section>

## 3. Cold waves: compound cooling and sustained cold are alternative routes

The cold-wave criteria apply during **October–April**. Each tier has two alternative routes in the current table.

<section aria-label="Alternative routes for cold-wave alerts" className="my-6 grid gap-3">

<div className="rounded-lg border border-border bg-card p-4">

### Cold-wave advisory

1. The morning minimum is expected to fall by **at least 10°C** from the previous day, reach **3°C or lower**, **and** be **at least 3°C below the climatological normal**.
2. Morning minima **at or below −12°C** are expected to persist for **at least 2 days**.

</div>

<div className="rounded-lg border border-border bg-card p-4">

### Cold-wave warning

1. The morning minimum is expected to fall by **at least 15°C**, reach **3°C or lower**, **and** be **at least 3°C below the climatological normal**.
2. Morning minima **at or below −15°C** are expected to persist for **at least 2 days**.

</div>

</section>

Conditions within the first route are joined by **and**; the two routes are joined by **or**. Do not omit the normal-temperature condition or judge the route from the drop alone. These cold-wave criteria use morning minimum **air temperature**, not wind chill.

### A hypothetical reading exercise

If a morning minimum falls from 8°C to −2°C, the 10°C drop and the 3°C ceiling are satisfied. Without the climatological normal, however, the first route cannot be confirmed. This is an editorial example, not a dated observation or an actual alert decision.

## 4. Include the severe heat tier and regional tropical-night criteria

A heat advisory concerns a daily maximum **apparent temperature of at least 33°C for at least 2 days** as expected; the warning uses **35°C for at least 2 days**. Unlike the cold-wave row, this row uses apparent temperature.

For the current **중대경보** tier, the prerequisite is a region where daily maximum apparent temperatures **of at least 35°C have been observed for at least 2 days**. In that region, either of the following must be expected:

- Daily maximum **apparent temperature of at least 38°C for at least 1 day**.
- Daily maximum **air temperature of at least 39°C for at least 1 day**.

Keep the observed prerequisite and forecast condition together. The 38°C and 39°C alternatives refer to different quantities.

The tropical-night advisory combines an expected daily maximum apparent temperature of at least 33°C for at least 2 days **and** a forecast nighttime minimum meeting the **regional threshold designated by KMA**. Do not replace this advisory rule with one nationwide nighttime temperature. Consult the regional tropical-night threshold guide linked on the same official page.

## 5. Read the actual bulletin before acting

Check the phenomenon, area, issue time and effective time. Identify the quantity, accumulation window and prerequisites, then consult the bulletin and instructions from the relevant authorities. One station reading, a model output, a disaster text message and an evacuation order serve different purposes.

Station counts, radar counts and satellite sampling intervals can change. Listing them without a dated source does not establish the alert decision, so they are not presented here as fixed operational facts.

## Summary

Read alert criteria as **quantity + threshold + duration + logic + region**. Rain windows use **or**; the conditions within the rapid-cooling route use **and**. Include the severe heat tier and regional tropical-night criteria, and do not use this explanatory chapter to determine whether an official alert has been issued.
