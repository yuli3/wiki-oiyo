A skeletal knight rides forward on a white horse, carrying a black banner. Even a king has fallen before him, yet the sun rises on the horizon. The Death card is frightening because of the "ending," but that ending is always paired with dawn.

## Reading the Symbols

The white rose on the banner symbolizes pure life force beyond death, the rising sun shows that an ending is also a beginning, and the armor represents a force of change that cannot be stopped. Thirteen is the number after 12, the completed cycle — the end of one cycle.

## Upright Meaning

It is time to release what has reached the end of its life. A relationship, habit, job, identity — whatever must end is in the process of ending. It may hurt, but once this clearing is complete, blocked energy will begin to flow in a new direction.

- **Love**: The ending of a relationship that needs to end, or the death of an old pattern in the relationship so a new stage can begin.
- **Work & Study**: A chapter transition such as resignation, career change, or the end of a project. The cleaner the ending, the better what comes next.
- **Money**: Clearing out an old spending structure. This is a time to cut fixed costs that need to be cut.

## Reversed Meaning

Death reversed shows a state of holding on to something already over. While change is delayed, stagnation turns into decay. The greatest pain comes not from change itself, but from resistance to change.

## Card Combinations

- **Death + The Fool**: A complete ending and a complete beginning. A powerful combination for a life reset.
- **Death + The Tower**: Sudden collapse rather than gradual closure. Emotional preparation is needed.

## FAQ

**Q. If the Death card appears, does it mean something bad will happen?**

A. In tarot, it is generally safe to say the Death card does not mean actual death. Its essence is "transition." In fact, when the question is about a stagnant situation, Death can be read as welcome news that things are finally moving.
