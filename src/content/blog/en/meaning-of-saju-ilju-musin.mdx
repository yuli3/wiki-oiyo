The Mu-Sin (戊申) Ilju is the 45th pillar of the sexagenary cycle. It represents the Heavenly Stem Mu (Yang Earth), a steady, immovable mountain, sitting upon the Earthly Branch Sin (Monkey), a creature of great talent. It is the image of a monkey performing tricks within a mountain—a heavy, grounded mountain that has acquired skill and dexterity.

## Personality and Disposition

The Mu-Sin Ilju is a versatile, active, and problem-solving type. The heavy, reliable nature of the mountain is combined with the talent of the Siksin (Eating God), meaning that if a task is entrusted to them, they will find a way to get it done.

- **Strengths**: Versatility, practical problem-solving skills, and blessings regarding food and livelihood.
- **Points of Caution**: Because they have many talents, they may struggle to focus on one thing. They will achieve great success if they choose one specialty and master it.

## Structural Analysis — Myeongri Interpretation

The [Mu (戊) Day Master](/en/meaning-of-saju-stem-mu/) sits upon the [Sin (申) Earthly Branch](/en/meaning-of-saju-branch-sin-monkey/). Based on the main qi of the Ilji (Day Branch), the Ten God is [Siksin (食神)](/en/meaning-of-saju-god-siksin/), and the 12 Stages of Life is [Byeong (病)](/en/meaning-of-saju-stage-byeong/). The Siksin is the star of expression and sustenance—the blessing of making a living by utilizing one's talents is embedded in the day pillar. The position of Byeong (Sickness) creates a structure characterized by deep sensitivity and empathy.

In terms of Nabeum, it belongs to Daeyeok-to (大驛土), a classification frequently used in traditional Myeongri alongside other pillars sharing the same Nabeum.
