An **emotion diary** is a journaling method where you choose **one event** from your day and write it down in five steps:

**Event → Thought → Emotion → Action → Outcome**

## Why This Order

This structure is rooted in the **ABC model** of cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT): Activating event – Belief – Consequence. Its core premise is that emotions do not come directly from events, but from **thoughts about those events**. Even the same event can lead to different emotions when the thought changes.

## How to Write One

1. **Event** — Write only the facts of one scene from today (no interpretation)
2. **Thought** — The automatic thought that passed through your mind in that moment
3. **Emotion** — Name what you felt then (more than one is okay)
4. **Action** — What you did as a result
5. **Outcome** — What that action produced

## Changing Perspective — The Second Half of an Emotion Diary

Once the record is complete, take one more step: **"Can I change the perspective from which I view this situation?"** Try applying a different thought to the same event, then write down what emotion arises when you look at it through that thought. When you confirm with your own hand that emotions change as thoughts change, emotion moves from "something that happens to me" to "something I can work with."

## Tips for Better Results

- One scene a day is enough — consistency matters more than volume
- Expanding your emotion vocabulary improves resolution (irritation, hurt, and helplessness are different emotions)
- It is especially useful when emotional suppression has become a habit ([smile mask syndrome](/ko/meaning-of-smile-mask-syndrome/))
