The Eul-Myo (乙卯) Ilju is the 52nd pillar of the sexagenary cycle. It represents the Heavenly Stem Eul (Yin Wood), a vine that flexibly winds its way up, sitting upon the Earthly Branch Myo (Rabbit), which signifies gentle growth. It is the image of wildflowers filling a spring field—a form where softness gathers in groups to become strong.

## Personality and Temperament

The Eul-Myo Ilju is a networking type that is both flexible and firmly grounded. Because it sits in the position of Geonrok (Established Prosperity), it secures its own territory in a gentle yet certain manner.

- **Strengths**: Flexible survival skills, wide social network, and practical self-reliance.
- **Points to Note**: There is a subtle but strong sense of competition among those with the same roots. Choose solidarity over comparison.

## Structural Analysis — Myeongri Interpretation

The [Eul (乙) Day Master](/ko/meaning-of-saju-stem-eul/) sits atop the [Myo (卯) Earthly Branch](/en/meaning-of-saju-branch-myo/). Based on the primary energy (Jeonggi) of the Ilji, the Ten God is [Bigyeon (比肩)](/en/meaning-of-saju-god-bigyeon/), and the 12 Stages of Life is [Geonrok (建祿)](/ko/meaning-of-saju-stage-geonrok/). Bigyeon is the star of the self and subjectivity—the power to stand on one's own is supported by the Ilji. As it is the position of Geonrok, it is a solid structure that relies on one's own competence to achieve independence.

In terms of Nabeum Five Elements, it belongs to Daegyesu (Great Stream Water), a classification often used in traditional Myeongri alongside paired pillars sharing the same Nabeum.
