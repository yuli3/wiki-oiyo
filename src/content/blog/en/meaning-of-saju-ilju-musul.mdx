The Mu-Sul (戊戌) Ilju is the 35th pillar of the sexagenary cycle. It represents the Heavenly Stem Mu—Yang Earth, symbolizing an unshakeable mountain—sitting atop the Earthly Branch Sul, the loyal dog. It is the image of a deep mountain in late autumn, standing firm and heavy in its place.

## Personality and Temperament

The Mu-Sul Ilju is a loyal, conservative, and protective type. Possessing the strong energy of the Gwaegang (魁罡), they see through what they have committed to until the end and often act as the "eldest brother" or leader within an organization.

- **Strengths**: Profound loyalty, unshakeable protective power.
- **Points of Caution**: Can be perceived as stubborn. Remember that even a mountain changes its attire according to the seasons.

## Structural Analysis — Saju Interpretation

The [Mu (戊) Day Master](/ko/meaning-of-saju-stem-mu/) sits upon the [Sul (戌) Branch](/en/meaning-of-saju-branch-sul/). Based on the main qi of the daily branch, the Ten God is [Companion (比肩)](/en/meaning-of-saju-god-bigyeon/), and the 12 Stages of Life is [Grave (墓)](/ko/meaning-of-saju-stage-myo/). The Companion is the star of the self and subjectivity—the power to stand on one's own is supported by the daily branch. The position of the "Grave" indicates a structure of management, gathering, and preservation.

In terms of Nabeum Five Elements, it belongs to Pyeongji-mok (Flatland Wood), a classification often used in traditional Saju studies to group pillars sharing the same Nabeum.
