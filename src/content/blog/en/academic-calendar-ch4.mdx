Delaying entry by one year is not the same procedure as obtaining deferment of compulsory attendance for unavoidable circumstances such as illness. Similar everyday labels hide different authorities and decision processes. A parent's application is not automatically a deferment decision.

This is a guide to South Korean arrangements, not admission rules abroad or an assessment of a child's health or development. Legislation was checked on 21 October 2026.

## 1. The normal entry year uses the calendar year

[Article 13 of Korea's Elementary and Secondary Education Act](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lsJoLnkSeq=1032275547) normally requires entry on 1 March of the year after the child turns six. A child born in 2021 turns six in 2027 and normally enters in 2028. The legal entry reference date is not necessarily the school's ceremony or first day of attendance.

Children with the same birth year share a normal entry year, not an unconditional guarantee of the same grade regardless of early or delayed entry. Unsupported stories about historical birthday conventions or developmental effects are not a basis for deciding the current procedure.

Early entry is in the year after the child turns five; delayed entry is in the year after the child turns seven. Calling these simply entry 'at five' or 'at seven' can misstate the child's age on the actual date. For birth year 2021, the early-entry application year is 2026, whereas the delayed-entry application year is 2027.

## 3. Match the request to the authority

[Article 15 of the Enforcement Decree](https://law.go.kr/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lsJoLnkSeq=1019542539) sets application periods, authorities and entry-list handling. The relevant year depends on the child's birth year; these are not two options open to every child every autumn.

<dl>
<dt>Early entry — 조기입학</dt>
<dd>A guardian applies to the head of the eup, myeon or dong between 1 October and 31 December of the year the child turns five. An eligible early entrant is added to the school-entry list.</dd>
<dt>Delayed entry — 입학연기</dt>
<dd>A guardian applies to that local authority between 1 October and 31 December of the year the child turns six. The child is excluded from the list compiled that year. This is not an application to a school for compulsory-attendance deferment.</dd>
<dt>Deferment or exemption — 유예·면제</dt>
<dd>A separate route when attendance is impossible for qualifying unavoidable reasons. Apply to the principal of the intended or current school. The principal decides after the compulsory-education management committee's deliberation; an application is not approval.</dd>
</dl>

This last route is governed by [Article 14 of the Act](https://www.law.go.kr/lsLawLinkInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lsJoLnkSeq=1000158914) and [Article 28 of the Decree](https://www.law.go.kr/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lsJoLnkSeq=1033325221). Deferment is for no more than one year, with renewed deferment or extension possible for special reasons through the prescribed procedure. Deferment and exemption are not the same result; do not treat either as a generic permanent permission never to attend school.

## 4. When a deadline has passed or illness is involved

<details>
<summary>Can a family simply wait a year after missing 31 December?</summary>

Not on that assumption. Missing the delayed-entry application window is different from satisfying deferment requirements. Contact the local office and intended school about the applicable process, evidence and decision notice. Non-attendance does not substitute for authorization. Article 28 reasons and evidence must be checked against the education superintendent's criteria and individual circumstances; do not assert that every case needs an identical medical certificate.

</details>

<ol>
<li>Calculate the normal entry year and the separate early/delayed application years from the birth year.</li>
<li>Distinguish a choice to enter one year earlier/later from an inability to meet compulsory-attendance obligations for qualifying reasons.</li>
<li>Confirm receipt, list changes, any deliberation and notification with the responsible local office or school.</li>
</ol>

## 5. Four distinctions across this course

The school-year boundary does not automatically decide personal enrollment status. A school's teaching days are not individual attendance or vacation length. International starting months need jurisdiction, school level and actual calendars. Early entry, delayed entry and compulsory-attendance deferment have different procedures. Keeping these distinctions prevents a calendar date from being mistaken for a child's legal status.
