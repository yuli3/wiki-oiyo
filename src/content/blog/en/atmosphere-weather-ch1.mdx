Airborne particulate matter is one of the most prominent environmental and public health concerns across East Asia. In public meteorology and air quality reports, dust is categorized primarily by its aerodynamic diameter: **PM10** (coarse inhalable particles) and **PM2.5** (fine respirable particles).

Understanding the physical scale and biological penetration mechanisms of these aerosols is critical for interpreting environmental alerts and selecting appropriate respiratory protection.

---

## 1. Aerodynamic Diameter and Scale Comparison

The designation "PM" stands for Particulate Matter, and the attached number denotes the aerodynamic cut-off diameter in micrometers ($\mu m$, where $1\,\mu m = 10^{-6}\,m$):

- **PM10 ($10\,\mu m$ or smaller)**: Coarse particles approximately 1/5th to 1/7th the thickness of an average human hair (which spans 50 to 70 micrometers in diameter). Examples include crushed mineral dust, pollen grains, and mold spores.
- **PM2.5 ($2.5\,\mu m$ or smaller)**: Ultrafine particles roughly 1/20th to 1/30th the diameter of a human hair. Examples include combustion aerosols from vehicle exhaust, industrial coal fly ash, and secondary aerosol condensation products ($SO_x, NO_x$).

```
[Human Hair: 50~70 μm] ──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  ├── [Fine Beach Sand: ~90 μm]                                      │
  ├── [PM10 Inhalable Dust: ≤ 10 μm] ───┐                            │
  │     └── Upper respiratory tract (nasal cilia, trachea filtration)│
  └── [PM2.5 Respirable Dust: ≤ 2.5 μm] ───┐                         │
        └── Alveolar penetration & systemic bloodstream entry        │
```

---

## 2. Biological Penetration: Nasal Cilia vs Alveolar Blood Barriers

The decisive physiological difference between PM10 and PM2.5 lies in how human anatomy handles airborne particulates:

1. **PM10 Filtration in Upper Airways**: Particles between $2.5\,\mu m$ and $10\,\mu m$ are predominantly captured by nasal hairs, mucosal secretions in the pharynx, and bronchial ciliated epithelia. They are expelled upward via the mucociliary escalator and cleared through coughing or phlegm.
2. **PM2.5 Alveolar Translocation**: Particles smaller than $2.5\,\mu m$ bypass upper airway filtration mechanisms entirely. They travel deep into terminal bronchioles and pulmonary alveoli, where gas exchange takes place. There, ultrafine soot particles penetrate alveolar epithelial walls, enter capillaries, and circulate systemically throughout the vascular system, triggering endothelial inflammation, arterial plaque destabilization, and cerebrovascular risks.

## 3. South Korea Ministry of Environment 4-Tier Warning System

South Korea maintains rigorous public alert thresholds benchmarked against the World Health Organization (WHO) air quality guidelines:

| Grade | PM10 Concentration (24h Average) | PM2.5 Concentration (24h Average) | Recommended Protective Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Good (좋음)** | $0 \sim 30\,\mu g/m^3$ | $0 \sim 15\,\mu g/m^3$ | Normal outdoor recreation permitted |
| **Moderate (보통)** | $31 \sim 80\,\mu g/m^3$ | $16 \sim 35\,\mu g/m^3$ | Standard outdoor activities |
| **Bad (나쁨)** | $81 \sim 150\,\mu g/m^3$ | $36 \sim 75\,\mu g/m^3$ | Wear KF80/KF94 masks; sensitive groups reduce outdoor time |
| **Very Bad (매우나쁨)** | **$151\,\mu g/m^3$ or higher** | **$76\,\mu g/m^3$ or higher** | Refrain from outdoor sports; vulnerable groups stay indoors |

When PM2.5 hourly concentrations exceed $75\,\mu g/m^3$ for two consecutive hours, a municipal **Fine Dust Advisory (주의보)** is issued; when exceeding $150\,\mu g/m^3$ for two hours, a **Fine Dust Warning (경보)** is triggered, restricting thermal power output and high-emission commercial transport.
