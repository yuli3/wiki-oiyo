**PM2.5 is part of PM10, not a separate chemical substance.** These labels describe overlapping aerodynamic size fractions. PM10 includes both fine and larger inhalable particles; PM2.5 is the finer fraction. See the [EPA definitions](https://www.epa.gov/pm-pollution/particulate-matter-pm-basics).

Understanding the physical scale and biological penetration mechanisms of these aerosols is critical for interpreting environmental alerts and selecting appropriate respiratory protection.

---

## 1. Aerodynamic Diameter and Scale Comparison

The designation "PM" stands for Particulate Matter, and the attached number denotes the aerodynamic cut-off diameter in micrometers ($\mu m$, where $1\,\mu m = 10^{-6}\,m$):

- **PM10**: inhalable particles generally 10μm or smaller, including PM2.5.
- **PM2.5**: fine inhalable particles generally 2.5μm or smaller. The term *fine* should not be replaced by *ultrafine*, a different size category.

| Illustrative object | Diameter | Ratio to an assumed 60μm hair |
|---|---:|---|
| Hair | 60μm | Reference for scale, not an airborne size fraction |
| Particle at the PM10 upper size | 10μm | 1/6 of the diameter; 1/36 of a circular cross-sectional area |
| Particle at the PM2.5 upper size | 2.5μm | 1/24 of the diameter; 1/576 of a circular cross-sectional area |

This is geometry, not a toxicity calculation. Actual particles vary in shape and density. A diameter ratio is not an area ratio.

Size, composition and formation are separate questions. Both fractions can include directly emitted particles and particles formed through atmospheric reactions. A universal percentage for secondary PM2.5 is not established by the size label; a measured share needs a location, period and method.

---

## 2. Biological Penetration: Nasal Cilia vs Alveolar Blood Barriers

The [EPA health overview](https://www.epa.gov/pm-pollution/health-and-environmental-effects-particulate-matter-pm) explains that particles below 10μm can reach deep into the lungs, and some may reach the bloodstream. That does not mean all PM2.5 bypasses filtration completely or enters blood directly. A size fraction cannot diagnose an individual's exposure outcome.

---

## 3. South Korean forecast grades are not warning thresholds

The [AirKorea forecast table](https://airkorea.or.kr/web/dustForecast) below uses daily-average concentrations. These forecast grades are not the same as statutory ambient standards or hourly advisory/warning criteria. Do not substitute one averaging period for another.

| Grade | PM10 (μg/m³, daily mean) | PM2.5 (μg/m³, daily mean) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Good (좋음) | 0–30 | 0–15 |
| Moderate (보통) | 31–80 | 16–35 |
| Bad (나쁨) | 81–150 | 36–75 |
| Very bad (매우나쁨) | Above 150 | Above 75 |

For the separate [advisory/warning criteria](https://airkorea.or.kr/web/pmWarning?pMENU_NO=115), AirKorea lists PM2.5 hourly means of at least75μg/m³ for two hours for an advisory, and at least150μg/m³ for two hours for a warning, considering local meteorological conditions. This statement does not establish the separate legal triggers for transport or power-generation restrictions.

Sources checked: 2026-10-18. The Korean forecast table is jurisdiction-specific even though the particle-size definitions are general. This chapter is not personalized medical guidance.
