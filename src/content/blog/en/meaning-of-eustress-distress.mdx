Endocrinologist **Hans Selye**, often called the father of stress research, divided stress into two types.

## Eustress

The term comes from the Greek prefix *eu-* (good), and refers to **a healthy level of tension that raises motivation and engagement**. The excitement of a new project, the heartbeat before a competition, and the focus created by a deadline all belong here. It overlaps with the "optimal arousal zone" described by the [Yerkes-Dodson law](/en/meaning-of-yerkes-dodson-law/).

## Distress

Distress is **negative stress that exceeds one's coping resources and leads to exhaustion**. It is marked by burdens that feel uncontrollable, have no visible endpoint, and leave no time for recovery. The accumulated state of distress is **burnout**.

## Same Event, Different Stress

The same presentation can become eustress when there is enough time and support to prepare, but distress when it exceeds what a person can handle. The character of stress is determined less by the event itself than by **the ratio between the event and available resources**. Interestingly, stress often grows not because of "what happened," but because of anticipation of "**what may happen**."

## The Goal of Management

Zero stress cannot be the goal -- a state with no stimulation at all can lead to lethargy. The goal is to **reduce distress while maintaining the rhythm of eustress**.
