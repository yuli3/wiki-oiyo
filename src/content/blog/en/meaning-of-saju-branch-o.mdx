Wu (午) is the position of the noon sun, representing high noon and midsummer. In the Twelve Earthly Branches, it is the point where Yang energy reaches its peak—a force that extends openly and without restraint, much like a horse galloping with its mane flowing in the wind.

## Personality Manifestation

Individuals with a strong Wu (Fire) influence are typically fiery, spontaneous, and expressive in their emotions. They thrive when in the spotlight, and like a running horse, they often feel frustrated by the need to stop or look back.

## Samhap and Chong

It forms a Fire-frame (火局) through the In-Wu-Sul (寅午戌) Samhap, and clashes with Zi (子) in the Zi-Wu Chong (子午沖)—a collision between the fire at its peak and deep water, often associated with emotional volatility and dramatic life changes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Are people born during the Hour of the Horse (11:00–13:00) impatient?**

A. The Wu (Fire) in the Hour Branch (時支) corresponds to one's later years or internal rhythm; rather than mere "impatience," it is viewed as a "peak time for energy." If the overall Saju chart has a balanced amount of Water (水), these individuals are able to maintain their passion while effectively managing their pace.

*Earthly Branches represent the 'time and environment' in Saju. The manifestation of the same character varies depending on whether it is located in the Year, Month, Day, or Hour pillar, and what kind of combinations (Hap) or clashes (Chong) it forms with surrounding characters.*
