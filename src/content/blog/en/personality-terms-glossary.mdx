When reading personality test results, terms like 'Openness', 'Neuroticism', or 'DISC' can trip you up. Here are the core terms you need, in one place. A personality test is a reference tool for self-understanding, not a way to reduce a person to a single type.

## Big Five (OCEAN)

The most widely researched model of personality, viewing it as five continuous dimensions.

| Factor | Core |
|---|---|
| Openness | Curiosity about new experiences, ideas, and art |
| Conscientiousness | Planning, self-control, responsibility |
| Extraversion | Sociability, activity, stimulation-seeking |
| Agreeableness | Cooperation, empathy, trust |
| Neuroticism | Sensitivity to anxiety (lower = more stable) |

## MBTI Four Dimensions

- **Extraversion (E)–Introversion (I):** direction of energy.
- **Sensing (S)–Intuition (N):** how information is gathered.
- **Thinking (T)–Feeling (F):** basis for decisions.
- **Judging (J)–Perceiving (P):** lifestyle.

## DISC Four Types

- **Dominance (D):** results-driven, challenging.
- **Influence (I):** people-oriented, persuasive.
- **Steadiness (S):** cooperative, patient, stable.
- **Conscientiousness (C):** accurate, analytical, rule-oriented.

DISC is a commercial behavioral tool that lacks independent peer review and should not be the sole basis for hiring or clinical decisions.

## Basics for Reading Tests

- **Temperament:** the innate emotional/reactive foundation on which personality forms.
- **Personality trait:** patterns of behavior and thought that recur across situations.
- **Reliability:** how consistently a test yields the same result.
- **Validity:** how well a test measures what it intends to.

Knowing the terms helps you read results more even-handedly. A result is not a verdict — it is a starting point for self-understanding.
