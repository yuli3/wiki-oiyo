<HighlightBox type="abstract" title="Definition">
  The **Ox** is the second animal in the Chinese zodiac and is paired with the
  Earthly Branch **Chou**. It is a symbolic and cultural cycle marker, not a
  deterministic statement about personality, fortune, or life outcome.
</HighlightBox>

## Cultural associations and traditional traits

The Ox is traditionally associated with steadiness, patience, endurance,
discipline, and reliable labor. These meanings draw on the agricultural role of
cattle across East Asian societies, where the animal became a symbol of work,
strength, and persistence.

In everyday zodiac language, Ox imagery may suggest a person who proceeds
carefully and values responsibility. It may also carry a folk warning against
stubbornness or inflexibility. Both sides are symbolic stereotypes, not
diagnostic facts. The Ox sign can be useful as cultural shorthand, but it cannot
measure temperament or predict achievement.

## Earthly Branch and cycle position

The Ox corresponds to **Chou**, the second of the 12 Earthly Branches. It
follows Rat and precedes Tiger in the standard animal sequence. In the
traditional calendar, Chou is one branch within a larger system that also uses
10 Heavenly Stems, forming 60 stem-branch combinations.

Representative recent Ox years include **1973, 1985, 1997, 2009, and 2021**.
These are common Gregorian-year labels. Because zodiac-year boundaries are tied
to traditional calendar rules rather than January 1, people born near the
beginning of a Gregorian year should check the exact calendar boundary used by
the relevant tradition.

## Compatibility folklore

Traditional compatibility folklore often places the Ox with **Snake** and
**Rooster** in a harmonious triad. Ox and **Goat** are commonly treated as a
clashing pair. These associations belong to symbolic matching systems and
should not be treated as rules for marriage, friendship, hiring, or family
judgment.

## Common Misunderstanding

The Ox sign does not determine whether someone is hardworking, conservative,
slow, loyal, or successful. It is a birth-year label in a cultural calendar
cycle. Complete East Asian fortune reading uses the Four Pillars, or saju,
which includes the year, month, day, and hour pillars. See the broader
[Chinese zodiac definition](/en/meaning-of-chinese-zodiac/) and the related
[Palja definition](/en/meaning-of-palja/) for the larger framework.

## Responsible use

Ox symbolism is best understood as cultural tradition, reflection, and
entertainment. It should not be used as deterministic prediction or as evidence
for judging a person's character, relationships, or future.
