The Byeong-Sul (丙戌) Ilju is the 23rd pillar of the sexagenary cycle. It represents the Heavenly Stem Byeong (Yang Fire), the sun that illuminates all things, sitting upon the Earthly Branch Sul (the Dog), which represents loyalty. It is the image of a sunset coloring the sky beyond the autumn mountains—a warm and graceful conclusion.

## Personality and Temperament

The Byeong-Sul Ilju is a storyteller with a warm heart and excellent expressive abilities. By sitting on the Sik-sin (Eating God), they are blessed with the capacity to give and share, and they often have a deep connection to the spiritual world, such as faith or philosophy.

- **Strengths**: Warm expressive power, a generous nature, and spiritual depth.
- **Points to Note**: They have a habit of dwelling on the past. Remember: the sunset does not block the sun of tomorrow.

## Structural Analysis — Myeongri Interpretation

The [Byeong (丙) Day Master](/en/meaning-of-saju-stem-byeong/) sits upon the [Sul (戌) Earthly Branch](/en/meaning-of-saju-branch-sul/). Based on the main Qi of the Ilji (daily branch), the Ten God is [Sik-sin (食神)](/en/meaning-of-saju-god-siksin/), and the 12 Stages of Life is [Myo (墓)](/en/meaning-of-saju-stage-myo/). Sik-sin is the star of expression, food, and clothing—the talent to provide for oneself through sharing is embedded in the Ilji. Because it is in the position of Myo (Grave), it is a structure of management, gathering, and storing.

In terms of Nabeum Five Elements, it belongs to Oksang-to (Earth on the Roof), a classification often used in traditional Myeongri to group pillars that share the same Nabeum.
