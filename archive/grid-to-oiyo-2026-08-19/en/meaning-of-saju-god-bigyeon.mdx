Bijeon (比肩) literally means "shouldering together" and represents a character with the exact same energy as the self. It symbolizes another version of 'me'—siblings, friends, business partners, and the power of an unshakeable ego.

## At a Glance

| Category | Description |
|---|---|
| Definition | Characters with the same element and yin-yang polarity as the self (Ilgan) |
| Governs | Self, siblings, colleagues, independence |
| Key Keywords | Subjectivity, independence, colleagues, competition |

## Personality Traits

People with a prominent Bijeon have clear convictions and do not rely on others. Regardless of what others say, they maintain their own pace and prefer equal relationships, disliking being suppressed by hierarchy.

## Career and Work

They excel in independent work, solo businesses, or professions where they can "work under their own name." In collaborative settings, synergy is achieved when there is a clear division of roles.

## Wealth

They are strong at earning money through their own efforts, but in partnerships or financial transactions, they must clearly define the boundaries between "mine" and "yours" to avoid trouble.

## Excess and Deficiency

When in excess, it can manifest as stubbornness and self-righteousness, and it creates a situation where competitors for wealth (Jaeseong) increase. When deficient, one's subjectivity may be weak, making it easy to be swayed by others.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Is it true that having many Bijeon makes marriage difficult?**

A. An excess of Bijeon simply indicates a tendency toward "strong independence that makes it difficult to compromise," not an impossibility of marriage. If one meets a partner who respects each other's boundaries, it can actually create the most clean-cut and egalitarian relationship structure.

---

*Sipsin is a language used to interpret the relationship between the Ilgan (the self) and other characters. The manifestation of the same Sipsin varies depending on its position, strength, and interactions (Hap/Chung); there is no such thing as a "good" or "bad" Sipsin.*
