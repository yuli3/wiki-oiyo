The Gi-Yu (己酉) pillar is the 46th in the sexagenary cycle. It consists of the Heavenly Stem Gi (Yin Earth), representing the soil of a field that nurtures all things, sitting atop the Earthly Branch Yu (Rooster), representing precision. It is the image of an autumn field awaiting harvest—the fruition where grains ripen.

## Personality and Temperament

The Gi-Yu pillar represents a meticulous, highly capable, and practical individual. With the combination of the Eating God (Siksin) and the Long Life (Jangsaeng) stage, their cultivated talents lead to tangible results, and they possess excellent work efficiency.

- **Strengths**: Sharp practical skills, consistency in achieving results.
- **Points to Note**: They tend to worry excessively and stress themselves out. Once the grain is fully ripened, learn to trust the process and wait.

## Structural Analysis — Myeong-ri Interpretation

The [Gi (己) Day Master](/en/meaning-of-saju-stem-gi/) sits atop the [Yu (酉) Earthly Branch](/en/meaning-of-saju-branch-yu/). Based on the main energy of the day branch, the Ten God is [Siksin (食神)](/en/meaning-of-saju-god-siksin/), and the Twelve Stages of Life is [Jangsaeng (長生)](/en/meaning-of-saju-stage-jangsaeng/). The Eating God is the star of expression, food, and clothing—blessings of making a living through one's talents are embedded in the day branch. Because it is in the stage of Long Life, this structure is often accompanied by new beginnings, learning, and the support of noble people (benefactors).

In terms of Nabeum (phonetic element), it belongs to Daeyeok-to (大驛土), a classification frequently used in traditional Myeong-ri to group pillars with the same phonetic resonance.
