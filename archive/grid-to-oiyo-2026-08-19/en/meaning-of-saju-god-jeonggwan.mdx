Jeong-gwan (正官), as the name "Right Official" suggests, is the energy that refines the self through legitimate norms. It is a *gilsin* (auspicious star) that symbolizes honor recognized by organizations and society, as well as the dignity one maintains for oneself.

## Personality Manifestation

Those with a well-developed Jeong-gwan are upright and responsible, and are known everywhere as "reliable people." They respect rules and procedures, and self-discipline is ingrained in their nature.

## In Career and Work

They grow most stably within institutional and prestige-oriented tracks, such as public service, large corporations, the legal profession, or teaching.

## In Wealth

Wealth follows a standard upward curve, aligned with position and seniority. It is the instinct and asset of Jeong-gwan to avoid money that compromises one's honor from the start.

## Excess and Deficiency

When in excess, one may become bound by appearances and reputation, suppressing personal desires and becoming indecisive. When deficient, one may enjoy more freedom but must find other ways to prove their weight and credibility.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. I heard that in a woman's Saju, Jeong-gwan represents the husband?**

A. While this is a traditional perspective in *myeongni* (four-pillars study), the modern standard is to interpret it more broadly as "the general social relationships that regulate the self"—such as workplace, position, and partnerships. Narrowing it down to a single spouse to determine fortune is outdated and does not fit the current era.

---

*The Sipsin is a language used to interpret the relationship between the Ilgan (the self) and other characters. The manifestation of the same Sipsin varies depending on its position, strength, and interactions (Hap/Chung); there is no such thing as an inherently "good" or "bad" Sipsin.*
