The Gye-Myo (癸卯) Ilju is the 40th pillar of the sexagenary cycle. It represents the Heavenly Stem Gye (Yin Water), symbolizing a gentle, soaking rain, sitting upon the Earthly Branch Myo (Rabbit), which represents gentle growth. It is the image of a light drizzle falling on a sprout at dawn in spring—silently nurturing life.

## Personality and Temperament

The Gye-Myo Ilju is a person of gentle and creative sensibility. With the combination of the Siksin (Eating God) and Jangsaeng (Long Life), their talents bloom naturally, and they easily win the favor of others.

- **Strengths**: Soft creativity, a likable personality, and blossoming talent.
- **Points to Note**: Like a flower in a greenhouse, there is a tendency toward prolonged dependency. One must "endure the rain" to grow deep roots.

## Reading the Structure — Myeongri Interpretation

The [Gye (癸) Day Master](/en/meaning-of-saju-stem-gye/) sits atop the [Myo (卯) Branch](/en/meaning-of-saju-branch-myo/). Based on the main Qi of the daily branch, the 10 Gods correspond to [Siksin (食神)](/en/meaning-of-saju-god-siksin/), and the 12 Stages of Life correspond to [Jangsaeng (長生)](/en/meaning-of-saju-stage-jangsaeng/). Siksin is the star of expression, food, and clothing—the blessing of making a living by sharing one's talents is embedded in the daily branch. As it is the position of Jangsaeng, it is a structure that invites new beginnings, learning, and the support of noble people (Gui-in).

In terms of Nab-eum (phonetic element), it belongs to Geumbak-geum (Gold Leaf Gold), a classification frequently used in traditional Myeongri to group pillars with the same phonetic resonance.
