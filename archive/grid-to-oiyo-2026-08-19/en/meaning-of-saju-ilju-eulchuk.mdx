The Eul-Chuk (乙丑) Ilju is the 2nd pillar of the 60 Sexagenary Cycle (六十甲子). It represents the Heavenly Stem Eul (乙), a Yin Wood symbolizing a flexible, climbing vine, sitting upon the Earthly Branch Chuk (丑), the Ox, which symbolizes silent endurance. It is the image of a flower piercing through frozen ground to survive the winter—delicate in appearance, yet possessing an unyielding tenacity.

## Personality and Temperament

The Eul-Chuk Ilju is the embodiment of vitality, hiding steel-like patience behind a gentle demeanor. They are adept at silently enduring hardships to build wealth and are known for seeing tasks through to the end.

- **Strengths**: Endurance in the face of adversity, frugal wealth management, and reliability.
- **Points of Caution**: Because they struggle to express their hardships, they are prone to internalizing stress, and once their mind is set, they can be incredibly stubborn.

## Structural Interpretation — Saju Analysis

The [Eul (乙) Day Master](/en/meaning-of-saju-stem-eul/) sits upon the [Chuk (丑) Branch](/en/meaning-of-saju-branch-chuk/). Based on the main Qi of the Ilji (Day Branch), the 10 God is [Pyeonjae (偏財)](/en/meaning-of-saju-god-pyeonjae/), and the 12 Stages of Life is [Soe (衰)](/en/meaning-of-saju-stage-soe/). Pyeonjae is the star of liquid assets, indicating an innate sense for managing money and opportunities. The position of Soe suggests a structure that prioritizes seasoned, internal stability over outward flashiness.

In terms of Nabeum Ohaeng, it belongs to Haejunggeum (海中金), a classification frequently used in traditional Saju analysis to group pillars sharing the same Nabeum.
