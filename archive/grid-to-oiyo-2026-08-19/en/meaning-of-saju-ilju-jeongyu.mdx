The Jeong-Yu (丁酉) Ilju is the 34th pillar of the sexagenary cycle. It consists of the Heavenly Stem Jeong (丁), a yin fire representing a candle illuminating the darkness, sitting atop the Earthly Branch Yu (酉), the precise and meticulous rooster. It is the image of a lamp in a jewelry store after dark—light reflecting off gemstones to create a brilliant, shimmering effect.

## Personality and Temperament

The Jeong-Yu Ilju is a sophisticated aesthete with an exceptional sense of beauty. They possess a natural talent for refining and decorating, and they have a bright affinity for wealth.

- **Strengths**: Outstanding aesthetic sense, sophistication, and a natural aptitude for managing wealth.
- **Points of Caution**: One must ensure that their inner substance matches their outward appearance. Vanity can cause the oil in the lamp to burn out too quickly.

## Structural Analysis — Saju Interpretation

The [Jeong (丁) Day Master](/en/meaning-of-saju-stem-jeong/) sits upon the [Yu (酉) Earthly Branch](/en/meaning-of-saju-branch-yu/). Based on the main qi of the daily branch, the Ten God is [Pyeonjae (偏財)](/en/meaning-of-saju-god-pyeonjae/), and the 12 Stages of Life is [Jangsaeng (長生)](/en/meaning-of-saju-stage-jangsaeng/). Pyeonjae is the star of fluid wealth—the instinct for circulating money and seizing opportunities is embedded in the daily branch. As it sits in the position of Jangsaeng, this structure is often accompanied by new beginnings, learning, and the support of noble benefactors.

In terms of Nabeum Five Elements, it belongs to Sanha-hwa (山下火); this classification was frequently used in traditional Saju studies to group pillars sharing the same Nabeum.
