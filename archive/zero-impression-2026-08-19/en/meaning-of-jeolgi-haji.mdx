Haji (夏至) is the 10th of the 24 solar terms, falling around June 21-22 when the sun reaches ecliptic longitude 90°.

## Meaning and Origin

Haji is the day when the sun stands highest and daylight is the longest of the year. It is the peak of solar energy — after this day, the length of daylight begins to shorten little by little.

## Customs and Foods

Around this time, people steamed and ate newly harvested potatoes called "Haji potatoes," and when drought struck, villages held rain rituals. It is also when the full heat of summer and the rainy season begin together.

## Saju and the Solar Term

Haji is a mid-qi (中氣), marking the middle of the lunar month. In myeongnihak, the boundary of the month pillar (月柱) is based on the 12 jeol (節), while the mid-qi is read as the point when that month's energy becomes most concentrated.

## Wisdom of This Time

A peak is also a turning point — the wisdom of Haji is to prepare for the next season at the brightest moment.
