# Emotional Eating: Eating for Feelings, Not Hunger

**Emotional eating** is the act of eating food not because of physical hunger, but **to regulate emotions**. Food may temporarily soothe uncomfortable feelings such as stress, anxiety, boredom, or loneliness, but guilt often follows soon after, making it easy for a cycle to form.

## Physical Hunger vs Emotional Hunger

- **Physical hunger**: Builds gradually, can be satisfied by many kinds of food, and stops at fullness.
- **Emotional hunger**: Appears suddenly, craves specific foods, often sweet or salty stimulation, and is hard to stop even when the stomach is full.

## Why It Happens

The stress hormone cortisol can increase cravings for high-calorie foods, and food temporarily stimulates the reward circuit through dopamine. In other words, emotional eating is less a matter of willpower and more like a **learned emotion-regulation strategy**.

## How to Work With It

- **Pause and name it**: Ask, "Am I hungry right now, or is this an emotion?"
- **Alternative strategies**: Create other ways to handle emotions, such as walking, breathing, drinking a glass of water, or writing briefly.
- **Reduce self-blame**: Recognize that one episode of overeating is not a failure, but a signal.
