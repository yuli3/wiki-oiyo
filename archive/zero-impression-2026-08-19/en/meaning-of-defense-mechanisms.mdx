**Defense mechanisms** are **unconscious psychological responses** that operate to protect the ego from anxiety, conflict, and stress. Sigmund Freud conceptualized them, his daughter Anna Freud systematized them, and psychiatrist George Vaillant classified them hierarchically by maturity.

## 4 Levels of Maturity

### Mature Defense Mechanisms
- **Sublimation** — redirecting impulses that are difficult to accept into socially valuable activities (turning anger into exercise or creative work)
- **Humor** — processing the irony of a painful situation through laughter
- **Suppression** — consciously setting feelings aside until they can be dealt with, without denying them

### Neurotic Defense Mechanisms
- **Rationalization** — reducing disappointment by creating a plausible reason ("Those grapes were probably sour anyway")
- **Repression** — pushing painful memories or feelings into the unconscious
- **Reaction formation** — acting in the exact opposite way from one's actual feelings (being overly kind to someone one dislikes)

### Immature Defense Mechanisms
- **Projection** — attributing one's own feelings to someone else ("They hate me")
- **Regression** — returning to behavior from an earlier stage of development

### Psychotic Defense Mechanisms
- **Denial** — refusing to acknowledge reality itself

## Why It Matters

Defense mechanisms are not inherently bad; they are **protective devices that everyone uses**. However, noticing which mechanisms you tend to rely on creates room to consciously choose more mature mechanisms, such as sublimation, humor, and suppression, in stressful situations.
