Buying a car in Korea for the first time — or after a long gap — can feel overwhelming. New cars and used cars have different procedures, and the initial costs (acquisition tax, insurance, registration) add up more than most people expect. Here's the complete process, step by step.

---

## Buying a New Car

### Step 1: Choose a Model and Get a Quote
- Visit the manufacturer's official website to configure your desired trim and options, then request a quote
- Visit a dealership or request an online quote (most major Korean brands now support online purchases)
- **Comparison tip**: Get quotes for 2–3 competing models in the same class before committing

### Step 2: Choose How to Pay

| Method | Description | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Full cash payment | Pay the total upfront | Best negotiating position for discounts |
| Installment loan | Monthly payments through an auto finance company | Interest applies |
| Lease | Monthly rental — return or purchase at end of term | Tax benefits for businesses |
| Long-term rental | Fixed monthly fee, return at end of term | Lowest upfront cost |

- Compare manufacturer financing (e.g., Hyundai Capital) vs. bank auto loans — banks are often lower interest
- Lease and long-term rental comparisons: [Carsyou](https://www.carsyou.co.kr) and similar aggregator sites

### Step 3: Sign the Contract
- Confirm in writing: **exact vehicle spec, options, color, estimated delivery date, deposit amount, and final payment conditions**
- Deposit (typically ₩100,000–₩300,000) — verify refund conditions if the deal falls through
- Clarify which extras are dealer-installed accessories (often marked up) vs. factory options

### Step 4: Get Auto Insurance Before Pickup (Mandatory)
- Insurance must be in effect **before you drive off the lot**
- **Comparison**: [Insurance Damoa](https://www.e-insmarket.or.kr) (operated by Korea's FSS — free, covers all major insurers)
- Mandatory minimum: Liability for bodily injury (대인배상 Ⅱ) and property damage (대물배상)
- Strongly recommended: Add comprehensive (자기차량손해) and personal injury (자기신체사고) coverage

### Step 5: Pay Acquisition Tax and Register
- **Acquisition tax rate**: 7% of the vehicle purchase price (4% for light vehicles [경차]; EV discounts available)
- Registration is handled by the dealership in most cases
- New license plate is issued at the time of delivery
- **Tax exemptions and reductions**: Check [Wetax](https://www.wetax.go.kr) for eligibility (disabled, EVs, veterans, etc.)

### Step 6: Take Delivery
- Inspect the vehicle thoroughly at delivery: exterior scratches, interior fit and finish
- Confirm you receive: registration certificate, warranty booklet, owner's manual, spare key, and included tools
- Verify PDI (Pre-Delivery Inspection) documentation

## Buying a Used Car

### Step 1: Find Listings
- **Encar**: [encar.com](https://www.encar.com) — Korea's largest used car marketplace
- **KB Chachacha**: [kbchachacha.com](https://www.kbchachacha.com) — backed by KB Financial Group
- **HeyDealer**: Dealer auction platform
- **Private sales**: Bunjang, Carrot (당근마켓) — cheaper but riskier; accident history check is even more critical

### Step 2: Check Vehicle History (Non-Negotiable)
- **CarHistory**: [carhistory.or.kr](https://www.carhistory.or.kr) — Korea's official vehicle history report service (paid, but worth it)
- Verify: accident history, flood damage, total loss history, number of previous owners, odometer tampering

### Step 3: Inspect in Person
- Always test-drive before buying — never purchase sight unseen
- **Exterior**: Look for paint color inconsistencies (sign of panel repair), check gap uniformity around doors and fenders
- **Underside**: Look for oil leaks, rust, and impact damage
- **Driving**: Listen for abnormal noises, vibrations, and test the brakes
- **Professional inspection service**: Encar Diagnosis, Autobel, or similar services cost ₩50,000–₩100,000 and are worth every won

### Step 4: Negotiate Price
- Check market rates: compare same year, same mileage vehicles on Encar and KB Chachacha
- 5–15% negotiation room is typical depending on accident history, mileage, and season
- Extended warranty terms can also be negotiated with dealers

### Step 5: Sign Contract and Pay
- Contract must include: plate number, VIN (차대번호), odometer reading, deposit amount, balance and handover date
- Request the **성능상태점검기록부 (Performance Inspection Report)** — dealers are legally required to provide this
- For payment: bank transfer is safest; if using cash for a private sale, always bring someone with you

### Step 6: Transfer Registration
- **Deadline: Within 15 days of purchase**
- Process: Handle through the dealer, or visit your local vehicle registration office yourself
- Required documents: Seller's notarized seal certificate (자동차 매도용 인감증명서), original vehicle registration, buyer's ID
- Acquisition tax: 7% of the transaction price — paid at time of transfer registration

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. What are typical auto loan interest rates?**
Manufacturer financing typically runs 3–7% annually. Bank auto loans are often lower — compare before deciding.

**Q. What incentives exist for EVs or hybrids?**
Reduced acquisition tax plus government subsidies (national + municipal). Check current amounts at [EV Korea](https://www.ev.or.kr).

**Q. Private sale vs. dealer — which is better?**
Private sale: cheaper, but no warranty and higher fraud risk. Dealer: more expensive but includes inspection and limited warranty.

> Car purchases involve large sums of money. Read every line of the contract carefully, and for any uncertain points, consult a professional before signing.
