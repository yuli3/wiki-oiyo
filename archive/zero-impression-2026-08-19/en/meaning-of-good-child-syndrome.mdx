**Good Child Syndrome** is a popular psychology term for a psychological pattern in which a script formed through the conditional approval of "I must be good to be loved" in childhood continues into adulthood. It is not an official diagnosis.

## Signals

- You cannot turn down requests
- You withdraw your own opinion when conflict seems likely
- You smile even when you are angry — an overlap with [smile mask syndrome](/en/meaning-of-smile-mask-syndrome/)
- You feel responsible for other people's moods

## Relationship to Eldest Son or Eldest Daughter Syndrome

The eldest child who takes on responsibility early within the family can easily make "being mature" part of their identity. Caring for parents' emotions, yielding to younger siblings, and swallowing the words "this is hard" can become a role that continues even after adulthood. Emotions suppressed for a long time often erupt **late and intensely**. For example, when someone suddenly cuts off contact with their parents after turning forty, people around them may be shocked and say, "They were never like that before," but in reality it can be the result of decades of accumulation.

## Ways to Move Out of It

1. **Start with small refusals** — practice saying "I can't this time" with low-risk requests
2. **Raise the honesty of emotional expression by 10%** — you do not need to pour everything out
3. **Separate the role from the self** — "the caregiving role" is something I can choose; it is not who I am
4. Use the boundary sense in the [Gestalt prayer](/en/meaning-of-gestalt-prayer/) as a reference
