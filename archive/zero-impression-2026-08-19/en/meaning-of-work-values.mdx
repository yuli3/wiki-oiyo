**Work values** are the conditions and principles a person considers important in work, such as the work itself, environment, relationships, rewards, and autonomy. Interests are closer to “What do I want to do?” Work values are closer to “Under what conditions do I want to work?”

Work values do not measure ability, aptitude, personality, or qualifications. A high value score also does not prove that a career is a good fit. Values are one source of evidence in career exploration, not a verdict.

## The six O*NET and CareerOneStop work values

The U.S. Department of Labor's O*NET data system and CareerOneStop Work Values Matcher use six work values.

| Value | General meaning |
|---|---|
| Achievement | Work that uses abilities and provides a sense of accomplishment |
| Independence | Work that allows independent decisions and trying one's own ideas |
| Recognition | Work with advancement, leadership, and recognition |
| Relationships | Work that helps others and supports ethical, friendly relationships |
| Support | Work where management and the organization support employees fairly |
| Working Conditions | Preferred conditions such as security, pay, safety, variety, and activity |

CareerOneStop's current Work Values Matcher is a **card-sort activity in which users rank 20 statements** about an ideal job. It connects the choices to these six values and is based on the O*NET Work Importance Locator (WIL). The O*NET WIL itself was **retired on June 3, 2024**; its materials remain available for historical and research use.

## How the OIYO activity differs

OIYO separately created an **18-item, six-dimension self-reflection activity**.

| | O*NET / CareerOneStop | OIYO |
|---|---|---|
| Dimensions | Achievement, Independence, Recognition, Relationships, Support, Working Conditions | Security, Achievement, Autonomy, Contribution, Creativity, Recognition |
| Format | CareerOneStop 20-card sort | 18 item responses |
| Role | Public career-exploration framework based on O*NET | Original OIYO reflection activity |

Similar labels do not make the two systems equivalent. The OIYO activity is not the official O*NET WIL and is not presented as a psychometrically validated or normed assessment. Scores should not be converted between the systems or used alone to claim career fit.

## How to use a result responsibly

1. Treat a high score as a prompt about current priorities, not a fixed identity.
2. Interpret it alongside experience, interests, skills, and life constraints.
3. Add occupational research, conversations with workers, or career counseling before a major decision.
4. Revisit the exercise when your circumstances change; priorities can change too.
