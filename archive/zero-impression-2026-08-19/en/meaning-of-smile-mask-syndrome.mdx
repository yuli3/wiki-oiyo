**Smile Mask Syndrome** refers to a **psychological state of anxiety where one is unable to express negative emotions** due to the compulsion to maintain a bright appearance. The term originated from observations by Japanese psychologist Makoto Natsume (夏目誠) in the 1980s and is a descriptive concept rather than an official diagnostic term.

## Signs

- Thinking that one must not let others see that they are struggling
- Being unable to speak after returning home from work
- Absorbing others' emotions like a sponge
- Smiling on the outside while feeling exhausted on the inside

It shares roots with **Good Boy/Girl Syndrome** (being bound by the rule that one must be "good" to be loved, even into adulthood) and **Eldest Child Syndrome** (where the role of taking on responsibility becomes one's identity). When accumulated over a long period, it can manifest as an emotional outburst—the moment when others say, "Why is someone who never acted like this behaving this way?" is an example of this.

## Directions for Recovery

The key is not to stop smiling, but to **create breathing room for your emotions**.

1. **Increase the honesty of your emotional expression by just 10%** — Instead of saying "I'm fine," try "I am a little tired."
2. **Create your own emotional safe zone** — People, spaces, or times where you can be without a mask.
3. **Prioritize physical recovery** — Emotional suppression is stored as physical tension.
4. **Intentionally secure time without a mask** — Even if it is just 10 minutes a day.
