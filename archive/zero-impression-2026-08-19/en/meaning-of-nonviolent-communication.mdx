**Nonviolent Communication** (NVC) is a communication methodology developed by clinical psychologist **Marshall Rosenberg**. Its core insight is bold:

> All human speech is ultimately one of two things—a **request** (please) or an expression of **gratitude** (thank you).

Even harsh and aggressive words are seen as clumsy requests stemming from unmet needs. By listening this way, space is created to treat the other person with compassion. "Ah, they are making a request right now."

## The 4 Steps of NVC

1. **Observation** — Stating facts without evaluation: "You arrived late to the meeting three times this week" (✕ "You are always irresponsible")
2. **Feeling** — Expressing emotions, not thoughts: "I felt anxious" (✕ "I feel ignored" is an interpretation)
3. **Need** — Identifying the need behind the emotion: "I want my time to be respected"
4. **Request** — Making a specific and actionable request: "Would you let me know in advance if you think you'll be late next time?"

## 6 Types of Failed Communication

From the perspective of NVC, the following ways of speaking sever connection: **Judgment, Blame, Coercion, Comparison, Entitlement, and Rationalization**. These six habits shift the focus toward the other person's faults rather than their underlying needs.
