Before any international trip, travel insurance is not optional — it's essential. A single hospital visit abroad can cost thousands of dollars. The right policy covers you for next to nothing compared to the risk.

---

## Why Travel Insurance Matters

Medical costs abroad without insurance:
- **USA**: Emergency room visit — $1,000–$5,000+ just to walk in the door
- **Europe**: Hospital admission — $500–$1,000+ per day
- **Southeast Asia**: Cheaper, but still entirely out-of-pocket without coverage

Your domestic health insurance does **not** cover you overseas. Travel insurance bridges that gap.

### Direct Purchase (Often Cheaper)
Major travel insurers — World Nomads, Allianz Travel, AIG Travel Guard, IMG Global — often offer lower rates when you buy directly from their websites rather than through aggregators.

**Tip**: Buying before your departure date is almost always cheaper than buying at the airport.

## 2. Coverage Items You Must Check

### ① Emergency Medical Expenses (Most Critical)
- **Minimum recommended**: $100,000 USD for most destinations
- **USA / Canada travel**: $500,000–$1,000,000 USD strongly recommended (medical costs are extreme)
- **Dental care**: Often excluded or limited — verify separately
- **Pre-existing conditions**: Most policies require a "look-back period" review — declare everything

### ② Emergency Medical Evacuation
- Airlifting you back to your home country can cost $50,000–$200,000+ out-of-pocket
- Verify this is a separate coverage item with adequate limits

### ③ Personal Liability
- Covers damage you accidentally cause to others or their property
- Minimum $100,000 recommended
- Especially important in countries with higher litigation risk

### ④ Baggage Loss / Theft
- Check the **per-item limits** — many policies cap smartphones and electronics at $300–$500
- Cash is typically excluded entirely
- **Required documentation for claims**: Police Report (must be filed at the time of theft)

### ⑤ Trip Delay / Cancellation
- Delays: Reimbursement for meals/accommodation if flight is delayed by 6+ hours (varies)
- Cancellation: Covers pre-paid non-refundable costs if you must cancel due to illness, injury, or covered events

### ⑥ Adventure Sports
- Standard policies exclude activities like skiing, scuba diving, motorcycling, and bungee jumping
- If you plan any adventure activities, **add a sports rider** before departure

## 3. Coverage by Destination

| Destination | Key Considerations |
|---|---|
| USA / Canada | Extremely high medical costs. Get maximum medical coverage ($500K+) |
| Schengen Area (Europe) | Travel insurance with €30,000+ medical coverage **required** for Schengen visa |
| Japan | Medical costs moderate but still significant. Standard coverage sufficient |
| Southeast Asia | Affordable healthcare but motorcycle accidents are common. Check sports coverage |
| High-Risk Countries | Check your government's travel advisories — coverage may be void in Level 3–4 zones |

## 4. How to Enroll

### Step 1: Enter Your Trip Details
- Exact departure and return dates (use actual flight dates)
- All destination countries including layovers
- Number of travelers
- Trip cost (if buying trip cancellation coverage)

### Step 2: Select Your Plan
- Compare medical coverage limits carefully
- Add riders for adventure sports, rental car damage, or cancel for any reason (CFAR) if needed
- Read the exclusions section — not just the highlights

### Step 3: Save Your Policy Documents
- Download the PDF policy certificate → save to cloud storage
- Screenshot the **24-hour emergency contact number** and save it on your phone
- Share the policy number and contact details with a family member at home

## 5. Filing a Claim: What to Do

### At the Scene (Do This Immediately)
1. **Medical**: Get itemized receipts, doctor's notes, and diagnosis in writing (English if possible)
2. **Theft**: File a **Police Report** within 24 hours — this is non-negotiable for claims
3. **Flight delay**: Get a written confirmation letter from the airline stating the delay duration

### After Returning Home
- Required documents typically include: diagnosis letter, original medical receipts, claim form, passport copy, police report (if applicable)
- Filing deadline: Usually **within 90 days** of the incident (check your specific policy)
- Most insurers allow online claims submission through their apps or websites

### Emergency Contacts to Save Before Departure
- Your insurer's **24-hour international assistance number**
- Your country's embassy or consulate in your destination country
- Global emergency line (e.g., US State Dept: 1-888-407-4747 or +1-202-501-4444 from abroad)

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Can I buy travel insurance at the airport on departure day?**
Yes, but online purchase before your trip is typically 10–20% cheaper and gives you more options.

**Q. Is my credit card's travel insurance enough?**
Credit card travel benefits are often secondary coverage with low limits. Always verify the medical coverage cap before relying on it.

**Q. Does travel insurance cover COVID or infectious disease?**
It depends on the policy. Many newer policies include pandemic-related coverage, but terms vary widely. Read the exclusions carefully.

**Q. What if I have a pre-existing condition?**
Most policies exclude claims directly related to pre-existing conditions unless you purchase a waiver. Some insurers offer specific pre-existing condition coverage — disclose everything honestly.
