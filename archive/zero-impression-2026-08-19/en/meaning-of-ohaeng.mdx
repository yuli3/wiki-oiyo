## 1. Origin: not elements, but "phases of change"

The Five Elements were formulated in ancient China to explain change in nature. Where the Western four elements (earth, water, fire, air) denote "fixed substances," Wu Xing is closer to the "phases" that flow from spring to summer, autumn, and winter — a verb rather than a noun.

## 2. The nature of the five forces

- **Wood (木):** expanding, growing force. Spring, morning, east, benevolence.
- **Fire (火):** blazing, radiating force. Summer, noon, south, propriety.
- **Earth (土):** mediating, embracing force. Between seasons, center, faith.
- **Metal (金):** refining, gathering force. Autumn, evening, west, righteousness.
- **Water (水):** storing, flowing force. Winter, night, north, wisdom.

## 3. Generating and controlling: two cycles

The heart of the Five Elements is two cyclical relationships.

- **Generating (sheng):** Wood→Fire→Earth→Metal→Water→Wood. Wood feeds Fire, Fire creates ash (Earth), and so on, each helping the next.
- **Controlling (ke):** Wood→Earth→Water→Fire→Metal→Wood. Wood breaks up Earth, Earth dams Water, and so on, each keeping the other in check.

Saju interpretation reads the balance of these two cycles — draining what is excessive and supplying what is lacking (the "useful god," yongsin).

## 4. Misreadings and proper understanding

The Five Elements are not a fortune-telling formula that declares luck, but a "framework for reading balance and cycles." It does not say "strong Metal is bad," but asks "with Metal strong, which force brings harmony?" It is most useful as a tool for self-understanding and restoring balance.

### Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q: Are the Five Elements the same as the Western four elements?**
A: No. The four elements are closer to "fixed substances," while the Five Elements emphasize a cycling "relationship of change" in which each generates and restrains the others.

**Q: Is it a problem if I lack one element?**
A: A missing force is not inherently bad. Saju looks at how to complement what is lacking; it does not treat absence as a defect.
