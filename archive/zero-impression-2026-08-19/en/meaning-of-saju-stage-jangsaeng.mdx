Changsaeng (長生) is the first stage of the Sibiunseong (Twelve Life Stages), representing the position of a life that has just been born into the world. Much like a newborn baby growing rapidly under the care of those around it, it is considered a lucky star (吉星) that symbolizes pure growth energy and the assistance of a nobleman (gu-in).

## Meaning and Symbolism

Changsaeng does not mean "living a long life," but rather "the beginning of a long life (生)." It is the energy of a seed that has germinated and sprouted its first leaves; though still delicate, its direction is clear and untainted. Therefore, Changsaeng is interpreted as the star of **newness, learning, and patronage.**

## Temperament of a Chart with Strong Changsaeng

People with Changsaeng in their Day Branch (日支) often retain a youthful, boyish or girlish impression even as they age. They learn quickly and naturally attract help from elders or those around them.

- **Strengths**: Curiosity and absorptive capacity, a likable first impression, and luck where noblemen (gu-in) appear to nurture them wherever they go.
- **Points of Caution**: Because they are accustomed to being protected, their self-reliance may develop later. While they are good at starting things, they may need to borrow the strength of other characters to see things through to the end.

## Encountering Changsaeng in Daeun or Se-un

When encountering Changsaeng in a Daeun (10-year luck cycle) or Se-un (annual luck), it is seen as a period when new studies, new fields, or new connections begin to sprout. Since this is a time for **planting seeds** rather than reaping immediate large results, the choices made during this period become the soil for the next decade.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Is a chart with Changsaeng always a good chart?**

A. While Changsaeng is clearly classified as a lucky star, Sibiunseong merely shows the "state of energy" of a character and does not definitively label it as good or bad. The manifestation of the same Changsaeng varies entirely depending on which pillar (Year, Month, Day, or Hour) it occupies and which Ten Gods (Sipsin) it is combined with.
