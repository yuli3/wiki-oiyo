Moving in the US has some distinct quirks compared to other countries — laws vary by state, your credit score heavily impacts your rental options, and you need to notify multiple federal and state agencies of your new address separately. Here's everything you need to handle, step by step.

---

## 1. Before the Move — Finding and Signing a Lease

### Types of Housing
- **Apartment**: Managed complex with a landlord or management company. Month-to-month or annual leases common
- **House rental**: Renting from an individual landlord. Often includes a yard, garage
- **Studio / 1BR / 2BR**: Classified by number of bedrooms (Studio = open-plan, no separate bedroom)

### What to Check in Your Lease Agreement
- **Lease term**: Typically 12 months — confirm start/end dates and renewal terms
- **Security deposit**: Usually 1–2 months' rent. State laws cap the maximum amount
- **Pet policy**: Pet deposits and monthly pet fees are charged separately
- **Subletting clause**: Many leases prohibit subletting without prior written approval
- **Early termination fee**: Understand the penalty before signing
- **Move-in checklist**: Document existing damage with photos immediately — crucial for getting your deposit back

### No US Credit History?
New arrivals often get rejected for apartments due to a thin or absent credit file. Options:
- Offer a larger security deposit (2–3 months)
- Get a creditworthy co-signer
- Prepay the first few months of rent upfront
- Search for landlords in ethnic community networks who are accustomed to international tenants

## 2. Planning the Move — Movers and Packing

### Choosing a Moving Company
- **Full-service movers**: They pack, load, transport, and unload — most convenient but expensive
- **Moving truck rental**: U-Haul, Penske, Budget — rent a truck and drive it yourself (cheapest option)
- **Portable containers**: PODS, U-Box — dropped off at your door, you load at your own pace
- For interstate moves: Always verify the company is **licensed and insured** through [FMCSA](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/protect-your-move)

### Long-Distance (Cross-State) Moves
- Budget for **auto transport** if you're not driving your car yourself
- Get at least 3 quotes — pricing is based on volume and weight
- **Tipping**: $20–$50 per mover after completion is customary for a small move

## 3. Moving Day — Walkthroughs and Verification

### At Your Old Address
- [ ] Complete walkthrough of entire unit, photograph and video all walls, floors, and fixtures
- [ ] Photograph utility meters (electric, gas) for final readings
- [ ] Return all keys, key cards, parking permits, and fobs
- [ ] Empty and clean out your mailbox
- [ ] Disconnect washing machine drain hose

### At Your New Address
- [ ] Complete **move-in checklist** form — document any pre-existing damage, sign, and give a copy to the landlord
- [ ] Verify all utilities work (electric, water, gas, heat/AC)
- [ ] Confirm internet installation appointment (schedule with your ISP 1–2 weeks in advance)
- [ ] Test smoke detectors and CO detectors (legally required in most states)

## 4. After the Move — Address Changes You Must Do

### ① USPS Mail Forwarding
- [USPS Mover's Guide](https://moversguide.usps.com) → $1.10 identity verification fee → mail forwarded for up to 12 months
- **Important**: This only forwards mail — update your address directly with important senders too

### ② Driver's License Address Update
- Visit your **state's DMV** (Department of Motor Vehicles)
- Deadlines vary by state: California 10 days, Texas 30 days, New York 10 days
- Most states allow online updates: [DMV.org](https://www.dmv.org) for all state DMV links
- **Moving to a new state**: You must get a new state driver's license within the specified window (typically 30–90 days)

### ③ Vehicle Registration
- **Same state**: Update your address with the DMV
- **New state**: Re-register your vehicle (deadlines typically 30–90 days from move date)
- Some states require a smog/emissions test before new registration
- Notify your **auto insurer** — rates may change based on new location

### ④ IRS (Tax Authority) Address Update
- File **Form 8822** by mail: [IRS Form 8822](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8822)
- Online updates are not available for this — mail only
- Critical if you're expecting a tax refund check — it will be mailed to your address on file

### ⑤ Social Security Administration (SSA)
- Update via [my Social Security online](https://www.ssa.gov/myaccount)
- Required if you receive any SSA benefits

### ⑥ Voter Registration
- [Vote.gov](https://vote.gov) → update to new address
- Your old registration doesn't automatically transfer — you must re-register in your new state/county

### ⑦ Banks and Financial Accounts
- Update each bank, credit card issuer, and investment account individually
- Mismatched addresses can delay card deliveries and trigger fraud alerts

---

## 5. Getting Your Security Deposit Back

- Each state sets its own deadline for returning deposits: California 21 days, New York 14 days, Texas 30 days
- Landlords may deduct for **cleaning and damage beyond normal wear and tear** — they must provide itemized receipts
- If there's a dispute: Most states allow you to sue in **Small Claims Court** without a lawyer (filing fees are low, typically $30–$100)
