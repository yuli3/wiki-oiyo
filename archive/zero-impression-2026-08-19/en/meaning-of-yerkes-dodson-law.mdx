The **Yerkes-Dodson Law** is the **inverted U-shaped relationship between arousal levels and task performance**, discovered in 1908 by psychologists Robert Yerkes and John Dodson.

## The Inverted U-Curve

- **Low Arousal** — Boredom, lethargy, and lack of motivation. This occurs when repeating tasks that are too easy, leading to a state of apathy.
- **Optimal Arousal** — The point where focus and immersion are at their peak. Moderate tension enhances performance.
- **High Arousal** — Anxiety, freezing, and impaired judgment. Excessive pressure causes performance to collapse.

## Differences Based on Task Difficulty

A key finding of the original study: **Simple tasks** can be performed well even at relatively high levels of arousal, whereas **complex tasks** show optimal performance at lower levels of arousal. This means that the more difficult the task, the more a calm state is required.

## Implications for Stress Management

This law teaches us that "zero stress" is not the goal. The objective is to **reduce distress (harmful stress) and maintain eustress (optimal stimulation)**. While burnout is the accumulation of distress, lethargy can be the result of a lack of stimulation.

> Source: Yerkes RM, Dodson JD. "The relation of strength of stimulus to rapidity of habit-formation." *J Comp Neurol Psychol.* 1908;18:459-482.
