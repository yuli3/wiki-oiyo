Jiewang (帝旺) is the fifth stage of the Sibiunseong (Twelve Stages of Life), representing the peak where energy is at its most vigorous. In terms of a human life, it is the prime of one's life—the moment when the King (帝) is at the height of prosperity (旺), making it the most powerful star among the Sibiunseong.

## Meaning and Symbolism

Jiewang represents the energy of a mountain peak. It possesses a broad perspective, quick decision-making, and the power to lead others. At the same time, the peak is a place from which there is nowhere to go but down. Therefore, the classic challenge for Jiewang is "mastering one's own strength."

## Temperament of a Strong Jiewang Chart

Individuals with Jiewang in their Day Branch (Ilji) are the type who thrive only when they are running the show, rather than working under someone else. They are strong in high-stakes situations and become calmer as crises intensify.

- **Strengths**: Decisiveness and boldness, charisma to lead an organization, and the resilience to become stronger in the face of adversity.
- **Points of Caution**: Self-righteousness and stubbornness. It is a warning sign when advice from others begins to sound like nagging. While strong energy does not easily bend, it can break.

## Encountering in Major (Daewun) and Annual (Sewun) Cycles

The Jiewang cycle is a period when influence and achievements reach their peak. It is an excellent time to take on major positions or expand a business, but it also requires the wisdom to prepare for the descent after the peak—namely, succession, diversification, and humility.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. I heard that having Jiewang in a woman's Saju chart means she is overbearing?**

A. That is an anachronistic interpretation. The essence of Jiewang is "self-directedness," which is independent of gender. In the modern era, it is appropriate to interpret it as a star of leadership and an independent career.
