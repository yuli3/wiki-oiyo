<HighlightBox type="abstract" title="Definition">
  **Chinese zodiac** refers to the 12 animal signs associated with the
  traditional East Asian calendar: Rat, Ox, Tiger, Rabbit, Dragon, Snake, Horse,
  Goat, Monkey, Rooster, Dog, and Pig. It is a cultural and symbolic system for
  naming cycles of time, not a deterministic prediction of personality or fate.
</HighlightBox>

## What the Chinese Zodiac Is

The Chinese zodiac is a 12-part cycle of animals known in Chinese as
**shengxiao** (生肖) and in Korean everyday language as **tti** (띠). It is widely
used in China, Korea, Japan, Vietnam, and other East Asian cultural contexts,
with local names, customs, and occasional animal variations.

In popular use, a person is often described by the animal assigned to their
birth year. In calendar theory, however, the animals are not a standalone
fortune system. They are mnemonic labels attached to the 12 **Earthly Branches**,
which are part of a larger calendrical structure.

## The 12 Animals

The common 12-animal sequence is:

| Order | Animal | Earthly Branch | Common Cultural Associations |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rat | Zi | resourcefulness, alertness, adaptability |
| 2 | Ox | Chou | patience, steadiness, endurance |
| 3 | Tiger | Yin | courage, force, independence |
| 4 | Rabbit | Mao | gentleness, diplomacy, sensitivity |
| 5 | Dragon | Chen | charisma, authority, vitality |
| 6 | Snake | Si | discretion, insight, strategy |
| 7 | Horse | Wu | movement, energy, freedom |
| 8 | Goat | Wei | care, aesthetics, cooperation |
| 9 | Monkey | Shen | wit, invention, flexibility |
| 10 | Rooster | You | precision, visibility, discipline |
| 11 | Dog | Xu | loyalty, protection, sincerity |
| 12 | Pig | Hai | generosity, ease, appetite for life |

These associations are folk and symbolic patterns. They are not scientific
personality measurements.

## Relation to the Sexagenary Cycle

Traditional East Asian calendars combine **10 Heavenly Stems** with **12
Earthly Branches** to form a 60-step cycle often called the sexagenary cycle.
Because 10 and 12 meet again after 60 combinations, years can be named by a
stem-branch pair such as **jiazi** or **eulchuk**.

The zodiac animal corresponds to the **Earthly Branch** side of that pair. This
means a zodiac animal alone is only one layer of the calendar name. For example,
two people born in different Rat years may share the Rat branch but belong to
different stem-branch years within the 60-year cycle.

## Relation to Saju and Four Pillars

Saju, also called Four Pillars or bazi, uses four stem-branch pairs: year,
month, day, and hour. The year animal is therefore only the most visible part of
a much larger chart.

In many popular calendars, zodiac years are associated with Lunar New Year. In
Four Pillars practice, the year pillar is often calculated by solar terms,
especially the beginning of spring period. This is why people born near January
or early February should not assume their animal sign from the Gregorian year
alone.

## Cultural Meaning Across East Asia

The zodiac is used in New Year customs, age-language, family conversation,
compatibility folklore, naming cycles, art, stamps, temple imagery, and everyday
identity. Korea's **tti** language, Japan's **eto**, and Chinese **shengxiao**
all show how a calendar system became part of social memory.

Local forms may differ. For example, some Vietnamese traditions use Cat where
Chinese and Korean lists usually use Rabbit. These differences are part of the
system's regional history, not errors.

## Common Misunderstanding

The most common misunderstanding is that the Chinese zodiac is the same as a
complete East Asian astrology chart. It is not. A birth-year animal is a broad
cultural marker, while a Four Pillars chart uses year, month, day, hour,
Heavenly Stems, Earthly Branches, Five Elements, and interpretive rules.

Another misunderstanding is that the zodiac predicts a fixed character. A
responsible reading treats the animal signs as folklore, symbolism, and
conversation prompts, not as evidence about what a person must be like.

## Responsible Use

The Chinese zodiac is a cultural and symbolic tradition used for reflection,
storytelling, family customs, and entertainment. It should not be used as a
deterministic prediction system, a basis for discrimination, or a substitute for
professional, legal, financial, medical, or personal judgment.

Use zodiac animals as cultural language, not as proof of destiny.

### FAQ

**Is the Chinese zodiac based only on birth year?**  
Popular use usually focuses on birth year, but the underlying calendar also
uses branches for months, days, and hours. Four Pillars analysis is much broader
than the year animal.

**Does the zodiac year start on January 1?**  
No. Popular zodiac years often follow Lunar New Year, while Four Pillars
calculation commonly uses solar-term boundaries. People born near the beginning
of a Gregorian year should check the relevant calendar method.

**Is Dragon more powerful than other signs?**  
Dragon has strong cultural associations with authority and vitality, but no
animal sign should be treated as inherently superior.
