<HighlightBox type="abstract" title="Definition">
  **Tarot** is a 78-card symbolic system made of 22 Major Arcana cards and 56
  Minor Arcana cards. It is best understood as a tool for reflection,
  storytelling, and pattern recognition, not as deterministic fortune-telling.
</HighlightBox>

## What Tarot Is

Tarot is a structured deck of symbolic images used in cartomancy, meditation,
creative inquiry, and self-reflection. A reading places images, positions, and
questions into a temporary narrative frame so that a person can examine motives,
fears, choices, timing, and possible consequences more clearly.

In OIYO's dictionary layer, tarot should be read as a symbolic language. The
cards do not replace evidence, professional judgment, or personal responsibility.
They provide images that can make an unclear situation easier to discuss.

## Deck Structure

A standard tarot deck contains 78 cards divided into two large groups.

| Part | Cards | Function |
|---|---:|---|
| Major Arcana | 22 | Large life themes, turning points, archetypal lessons |
| Minor Arcana | 56 | Everyday situations, emotions, actions, conflicts, resources |

The **Major Arcana** runs from The Fool through The World in many modern decks.
These cards are often read as major symbolic thresholds: beginning, desire,
authority, love, crisis, insight, renewal, and completion.

The **Minor Arcana** is divided into four suits. Each suit has ten numbered cards
and four court cards: Page, Knight, Queen, and King.

| Suit | Common Element | Typical Field of Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Wands | Fire | Will, work, creativity, initiative |
| Cups | Water | Emotion, relationship, memory, intuition |
| Swords | Air | Thought, speech, conflict, decision |
| Pentacles | Earth | Body, money, craft, material life |

This structure matters because tarot is not a random set of pictures. The deck
combines sequence, suit, number, rank, and image.

## How a Reading Works

A reading usually begins with a **querent**, the person bringing the question.
The reader may be another person or the querent reading for themselves. The
question is framed, the deck is shuffled, and cards are placed into a **spread**:
a layout where each position has a role.

Common spread positions include:

- **Present situation:** what is active now
- **Challenge:** what blocks or complicates the matter
- **Advice:** what attitude or action may help
- **Outcome:** one possible development if current patterns continue

Interpretation depends on the card, the spread position, the question, and the
surrounding cards. The same card can have different emphasis in different
contexts.

## History in Brief

Tarot began as a card-game tradition in early modern Europe, with important
early evidence from 15th-century northern Italy. The cards later developed
regional forms, including the tradition now called the **Tarot de Marseille**,
whose images shaped many later European tarot decks.

The use of tarot for divination and esoteric interpretation became prominent in
the late 18th and 19th centuries. Modern English-language tarot was strongly
shaped by the **Rider-Waite-Smith** deck, first published in 1909 by William
Rider & Son, designed under A. E. Waite's direction and illustrated by Pamela
Colman Smith. Its fully illustrated Minor Arcana made everyday scenes central to
interpretation, which is one reason it remains a reference point for many
contemporary decks.

## Common Misunderstanding

The most common misunderstanding is that tarot predicts a fixed future. It does
not. A responsible tarot reading treats the cards as a mirror for reflection, not
as a command from fate.

Tarot can help someone name a pattern, compare options, or notice an emotional
assumption. It cannot guarantee events, diagnose a condition, identify hidden
facts with certainty, or remove the need for practical action.

## Responsible Use

Tarot is for entertainment, symbolic reflection, journaling, and personal
meaning-making. It is not medical, financial, or legal advice. It is not a
substitute for qualified professionals, evidence-based care, emergency support,
or informed decision-making.

Use tarot as a prompt, not as deterministic prophecy.

### FAQ

**Is tarot the same as oracle cards?**  
No. Tarot has a relatively stable 78-card structure with Major Arcana, Minor
Arcana, four suits, and court cards. Oracle decks can use almost any number of
cards and any structure chosen by the creator.

**Do reversed cards always mean something bad?**  
No. Reversals can indicate blockage, delay, inward expression, excess, or an
alternative angle. Their meaning depends on the reader's method and the spread.

**Can tarot make decisions for me?**  
No. Tarot can frame a question and surface themes, but the decision belongs to
the person using the reading.
