<HighlightBox type="abstract" title="Definition">
  **Blood-type personality** is the folk belief that ABO blood groups can
  describe a person's character or relationship style. It is popular in parts of
  East Asia, especially Japan and Korea, but it has **no reliable scientific
  basis** and should be understood as cultural folklore and entertainment, not
  psychology.
</HighlightBox>

## What Blood-Type Personality Is

Blood-type personality theory links the four ABO blood groups, A, B, O, and AB,
with everyday personality stereotypes. In Korea and Japan, blood type may appear
in casual conversation, dating profiles, entertainment shows, manga, celebrity
profiles, and playful compatibility talk.

The belief is culturally visible, but visibility is not evidence. ABO blood type
is medically important for transfusion compatibility and related biological
contexts. It is not a reliable tool for measuring personality, predicting
behavior, or evaluating character.

## Folk Associations

The following table describes common folk associations, not scientific facts.

| ABO Type | Common Folk Association | Careful Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| A | organized, careful, considerate, sensitive | a cultural stereotype, not a verified trait profile |
| B | independent, creative, direct, unpredictable | a popular label, not a personality diagnosis |
| O | outgoing, confident, practical, leadership-oriented | a social shorthand, not evidence of ability |
| AB | rational, unusual, private, dual-natured | a folklore pattern, not a psychological category |

These associations vary by source and community. They should not be used to
judge someone's competence, trustworthiness, compatibility, or emotional depth.

## Cultural History in Brief

ABO blood groups were identified in the early 20th century by Karl Landsteiner,
work that became foundational for modern transfusion medicine. Personality
claims about blood type developed separately and should not be confused with
medical blood typing.

In Japan, early 20th-century writing attempted to connect blood type with
temperament. The idea later became widely popular through mass media, especially
after publications by Masahiko Nomi in the 1970s. Korean popular culture adopted
and adapted the idea, often using it as a light social category similar to a
horoscope or personality quiz.

## Why It Is Not Science

Reliable personality claims require consistent measurement, strong evidence,
replication, and plausible interpretation. Blood-type personality theory does
not meet that standard. Large and critical reviews have not established ABO
blood type as a dependable predictor of personality.

The belief can feel convincing because of ordinary cognitive effects:

- **Barnum effect:** broad descriptions feel personally accurate.
- **Confirmation bias:** people remember matches and ignore mismatches.
- **Social learning:** people may perform the label they have heard repeatedly.
- **Stereotype pressure:** expectations can shape how others interpret behavior.

## Common Misunderstanding

The main misunderstanding is that blood-type personality is a branch of biology
or psychology. It is not. ABO blood type matters medically, but the personality
theory attached to it has no reliable scientific basis.

Another misunderstanding is that the belief is harmless in every context. It may
be playful among consenting friends, but it becomes harmful when used for hiring,
dating pressure, bullying, exclusion, or serious judgment about a person.

## Responsible Use

Blood-type personality theory should be treated as cultural folklore,
entertainment, or a conversation game. It should not be presented as science,
psychology, assessment, diagnosis, or evidence about someone's real personality.

Do not use blood type to make medical, financial, legal, educational,
relationship, employment, or safety decisions. Do not use it to label,
discriminate against, or pressure people.

Use the idea as folklore only, not as a prediction of who someone is.

### FAQ

**Can blood type predict personality?**  
No. There is no reliable scientific basis for using ABO blood type to predict
personality.

**Why is the belief popular in Korea and Japan?**  
It works as an easy social shorthand in media and conversation, much like
horoscopes or personality quizzes. Popularity does not make it scientifically
valid.

**Is ABO blood type medically real?**  
Yes. ABO blood groups are medically important for transfusion and compatibility
contexts. The unsupported part is the claim that they predict personality.
